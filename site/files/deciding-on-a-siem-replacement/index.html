30-second brief

Who
A CISO at a payments company
The decision
Deciding on a SIEM replacement.
What was at stake
Budget constraints made defending a 28% increase to the CFO untenable.
What they chose
Dana chose NightOwl mainly for its 3am support despite lacking features.
What happened
Migration cost $110k and took six months instead of two due to unforeseen complications.
Core lesson
Ensure all critical factors are properly weighted to avoid them becoming de facto decision points later.

03 Decision timeline

Nine months of trials and comparisons ended with one question — “who picks up at 3am?” — and then migration ran four months long.

MARCH · what started it

Renewal quote jumps 28%

Pricing model changed; at our volume it was untenable, so we went to market.

MAY–DECEMBER 9 months

Head‑to‑head evaluation and trials

Two full head‑to‑head trials; ~60 hours of team time.

MID‑EVALUATION 6 weeks

Seriously considered building it

Modeled an open‑source stack but killed it due to staffing reality.

JANUARY · the tipping point one meeting

The 3am question decides it

One meeting — only NightOwl had a named 3am engineer at our tier.

POST‑DECISION 6 months (4 months late)

Migration ran long

Took six months instead of two; four months late.

MAY · how it ended

Go‑live on NightOwl

Live in May instead of January.

Source: Dana Whitfield (CISO) and team — interview transcript.

The incumbent fell on price; Sentrix fell on real‑world use; Corvid fell on support at budget; NightOwl won for the 3am named engineer.

06 What we skipped

Skipping reference calls is what bit them — a few calls would have surfaced the 4‑month migration overrun risk.

Formal RFPSkipped Cost nothing — we went straight to vendors on procurement's approved list.
