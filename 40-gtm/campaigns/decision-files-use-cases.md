# Locked use cases — first three Decision Files

**Date:** 2026-08-15 · Each one names the widget it exercises, so the app build has a real target.

---

## 1. Which AI models and harnesses to standardise on — **running now**

**The decision:** every dev team is using three or four models and harnesses at once. COOs and
CTOs have to pick what the company actually standardises on, and nobody has criteria.

**Who to interview:** COOs, CTOs, VPs Engineering at companies that have made or ducked this
call in the last 12 months. Buyers, not vendors.

**Core analysis: stated vs. actual.** What they said they'd judge on — benchmarks, price per
token, context window — against what actually decided it. Expect the real answer to be
switching cost, what the team already knew, or one person's preference.

**Widgets:** `StatedVsActual` (primary) · `FeatureMatrix` (four-state — most answers here are
"partly" or "only on the higher tier") · `CostByProblem` where they can split spend.

**Why it's first:** it's live, it's unresearched, and **the buyers are model and tool vendors
with real budget** who are desperate to know how this call gets made.

**Note the lane:** this is the operator/general lane, not security. It may still be the right
first file — the topic is hotter and the vendor buyers are richer. Worth deciding on purpose
rather than by momentum.

## 2. What the Sullivan verdict changed

**The decision:** how CISOs decide under personal liability — who gets looped in, what gets
written down, where the line sits between protecting the company and protecting yourself.

**Who to interview:** 1 CISO for altitude + 2–3 architects/deputies per file. Offer the
role-only anonymity tier unprompted.

**Widgets:** `StatedVsActual` on informal criteria · `WhatWeSkipped` for anyone who has made a
disclosure call under time pressure.

## 3. Why did you cancel Gartner

**The decision:** dropping an analyst subscription and where the information comes from
instead. Hard budget filter — only a subscriber can answer.

**Who to interview:** whoever held the subscription line. Often a CIO or vendor-management
function, not the CISO. Follow the budget.

**Widgets:** `DropoutList` (what replaced it, what got dropped) · `StatedVsActual` (what the
subscription was for vs. what it was actually used for) · `CostByProblem`.

**Why it matters:** closest thing to a forced-participation lever — publish how buyers discover
vendors and every vendor needs to know whether they appeared.

## What this gives the app build

Files 1–3 exercise `StatedVsActual`, `FeatureMatrix`, `CostByProblem`, `DropoutList` and
`WhatWeSkipped` — five of the eight widgets, driven by real transcripts rather than mock data.
Build those five first; `DecisionTimeline`, `CutAndKept` and `CostCrossover` can wait for a file
that needs them.
