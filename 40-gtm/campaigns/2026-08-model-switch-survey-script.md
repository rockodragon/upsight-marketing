# Survey + Interview Script — "How did you actually decide?"

**Objective:** Learn how practitioners actually decide whether to switch the AI model their work is built on — the trigger, the comparison method, the true switching cost, and the one factor that tipped it.
**Target:** People who have shipped something that runs on a model and have either switched or seriously evaluated switching. Skew recruiting toward technical consultants, fractional operators, and agency/for-hire builders.
**Time:** Tier 1 — under 3 minutes. Tier 2 — 12–15 minutes.
**Method:** Tier 1 public ask via UpSight (LinkedIn + X). Tier 2 conversational UpSight interview (voice, video, or chat — respondent's choice).

> **Standing rule for both tiers: never name a vendor first, and never state Rick's leaning.**
> Let respondents introduce the names. If we frame it as a vendor contest we get
> loyalty performance instead of reasoning — see the campaign brief.

---

## TIER 1 — The public ask

### Post copy

> Genuine question, and I'll publish what I learn.
>
> If you've built something that runs on an AI model — think about the last time
> you switched the model underneath it, or seriously looked and decided not to.
>
> **What actually decided it?**
>
> Not what you'd recommend. What tipped *that* call.
>
> I'm collecting answers properly (voice, video, or text — whatever's easiest) and
> I'll publish the themes, the quotes, and the decision I make off the back of it.
> 90 seconds → [link]

*Production note: LinkedIn shows ~2 lines before "see more." "Genuine question, and I'll publish what I learn." carries that job — it promises a return on the reader's 90 seconds.*

### Q1 (the one question)
> Think about the last time you switched the model your work runs on — or seriously
> looked and stayed put. What actually decided it?

*Type: open-ended (voice / video / text)*
*Insight target: the real decision criterion, anchored to a specific past event rather than a general preference. "Or stayed put" is load-bearing — without it we only hear from switchers and the sample is broken.*

### Q2 (optional depth — the AI interviewer's follow-up)
Fires only if Q1 is thin. One of:
> — What were you running on it at the time? What would have broken if you got it wrong?
> — Was there a specific moment, or did it build up?
> — What did you check before you committed?

*Insight target: converts a one-liner into usable evidence. This is also the on-camera proof that a form can't do what we do — capture it.*

### Q3 (tagging — required, not optional)
> Which is closest to how you work?
> Independent consultant / fractional · Agency or studio (client work) · In-house at a company · Founder building my own product · Something else

*Type: multiple choice*
*Insight target: this is the ICP read. Do not skip it and do not bury it — it's the number that tells us whether this audience is anywhere near a buyer.*

### Q4 (closer)
> Anything else about that decision you wish someone had told you first?

*Type: open-ended, optional*
*Insight target: the standard catch-all. In practice this is where the best pull-quote usually comes from.*

**Then:** permission line — *"Mind if I quote you by name when I publish this? (Yes / Initials only / Anonymous)"*

## TIER 2 — Named practitioner interview (12–15 min)

### Introduction (read or send ahead)

> Thanks for doing this. Short version: I've got a real decision to make about the
> model my own work runs on, and rather than decide it in a vacuum I'm asking people
> whose judgment I trust how they actually made the same call.
>
> There's no right answer here and I'm not testing you on anything — I'm after how
> you actually did it, including the messy parts. I'll publish the themes and I'll
> send you anything I quote before it goes out.
>
> Fifteen minutes, and I'll stop at fifteen.

*Note: says nothing about which way Rick is leaning, and nothing about UpSight. Both would contaminate the answers.*

---

### Section 1 — The trigger

#### Q1
> Take me back to the last time you switched the model underneath something you'd
> built — or seriously evaluated it and didn't. What was going on that made you look?

*Type: open-ended*
*Insight target: the real switching trigger. Past behavior, specific event, no embedded assumption about what "should" prompt a switch.*
*Follow-ups: "When was that, roughly?" · "Was that a single moment, or had it been building?" · "Was anyone else pushing for it, or was that you?"*

#### Q2
> What did you have running on it at that point — and what was actually at stake if
> you got the call wrong?

*Type: open-ended*
*Insight target: magnitude and switching cost. Separates "I changed a default in a side project" from "this was in front of clients." Without this, every answer weighs the same and the synthesis is garbage.*
*Follow-ups: "Was that client-facing?" · "What would have been the visible failure?"*

---

### Section 2 — The method (the money section)

#### Q3
> Walk me through how you actually compared them. What did you run, read, or try?

*Type: open-ended*
*Insight target: **the central question.** Formal evals vs. a weekend of vibes vs. someone's post. This is where the piece's whole thesis will come from, and it's the one thing people don't publish about themselves.*
*Follow-ups: "Did you write anything down?" · "How long did that take, start to finish?" · "If you had to do it again, what would you skip?"*

#### Q4
> What surprised you? Something you expected to matter that turned out not to — or
> the other way round.

*Type: open-ended*
*Insight target: disconfirming evidence. Deliberately invites the respondent to contradict their own priors, which is where the non-obvious findings live. Also the single best source of a quotable line.*

#### Q5
> Where did you land — and what was the last thing that tipped it?

*Type: open-ended*
*Insight target: the decisive factor, isolated. "The last thing that tipped it" gets a single concrete cause instead of a tidy retrospective list.*
*Follow-ups: "Was that the reason at the time, or the reason in hindsight?"*

---

### Section 3 — The cost and the aftermath

#### Q6
> What did it cost you? Time, rework, anything that broke.

*Type: open-ended*
*Insight target: quantifies real switching cost. Most public discourse ignores this entirely, so it's differentiated content, and it's the thing Rick most needs for his own decision.*
*Follow-ups: "How long until it was fully settled?" · "Did anything break in front of a client?" · "Anything you'd still call unfinished?"*

#### Q7
> Since then, what's moved your confidence in either direction — and what are you
> watching now?

*Type: open-ended*
*Insight target: switching triggers, without asking a hypothetical. "What are you watching" is a present-tense behavior question; "what would make you switch" would be a guess and worth nothing.*

---

### Section 4 — Sources

#### Q8
> Before you decided — whose read on this did you actually check? Not who's loudest,
> who you actually trust.

*Type: open-ended*
*Insight target: two jobs. It maps where this audience really gets information (directly usable for distribution), and it hands us the Tier-2 recruiting list — every name here is a warm intro request.*
*Follow-ups: "Would you introduce me?" · "Anywhere you'd have looked and didn't?"*

---

### Closing

> That's everything I planned to ask. Anything I should have asked and didn't?

Then:
- Thank them.
- **Permission:** *"Two asks — can I quote you by name, and can I use a clip of that
  bit about [specific thing they said]? I'll send you both before anything goes out."*
- **Return the favor:** *"I'll send you the full synthesis before I publish — you'll
  see what the other N people said before anyone else does."* This is the honest
  reason a busy person says yes, and it costs nothing.

## Interviewer notes

**What to listen for**
- **The gap between the stated reason and the real one.** People give the defensible
  reason first ("benchmark scores," "cost per token") and the real one at minute
  eleven ("honestly, one of my clients asked for it by name"). Q4 and Q6 are the
  crowbars. Don't accept the first answer to Q5.
- **Method, not verdict.** If someone starts arguing for a vendor, steer back with
  *"what did you actually run?"* Verdicts are cheap and the internet is full of them;
  methods are scarce and are the reason this piece is worth reading.
- **Anyone who evaluated and stayed put.** These are the rarest and most valuable
  responses — the whole public conversation is switchers. Chase them.

**Traps specific to this topic**
- **Tribal performance.** People defend a vendor in public the way they defend a
  team. Antidote: never ask "which is better," always ask "what did you do."
- **Recency capture.** Whatever launched in the last two weeks will dominate every
  answer. Antidote: Q1 anchors to a specific dated event before anything else is
  discussed.
- **Rick's leaning leaking.** If he telegraphs which way he's going, half the room
  agrees to be agreeable and half argues for sport. Neither is data. Say nothing
  until publication.
- **Jargon drift.** Let the respondent's own words stand — "it got dumber," "it kept
  ignoring my system prompt." Don't translate those into cleaner language during the
  interview. The unpolished phrasing is the quotable part.

**Time discipline**
Sections 1 and 2 are the ones that matter. If you're at minute ten, skip Q7 and go
straight to Q8 and the close. Ending on time is how you get the second conversation.
