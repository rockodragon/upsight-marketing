## The one-liner

Every small company is committing to an AI stack right now, and almost none of
them can tell you how they decided. We interview operators — owners, COOs,
fractional operators — about **what they actually checked before they committed**,
and publish the criteria set they wish they'd had.

Not "which tool won." **How they decided, and what they'd check first next time.**

## Why this replaced v1

v1 ran the same motion on Rick's own model-switching decision. It would have
worked, but it recruited engineers — an audience we can't sell to — and spent our
only public-survey slot borrowing their attention.

This version changes one thing and fixes three:

| | v1 (model switching) | v2 (AI stack decision) |
|---|---|---|
| Who answers | Engineers, AI-Twitter | Owners, COOs, fractional operators — **the ICP** |
| What an interview produces | A quote | **A discovery call with a buyer** |
| The cold ask | "Want a demo?" | **"Can I interview you for a piece I'm publishing?"** |
| Shelf life of the output | Weeks — vendor stats decay | Months — a criteria set is evergreen |
| Rick's position | Outsider surveying a scene | **A peer making the same call** |

The unlock: **the Apollo consultant list and the interview recruiting list are the
same list.** Every Tier-2 interview is a qualified conversation with someone who
fits the ICP, ends with them having watched UpSight work on their own words, and
earns a natural second meeting. In v1 the interviews were content. Here they're
the sales motion, and the content is the by-product.

## What we're actually after — criteria, not verdicts

The published deliverable is **the criteria set**: the things operators actually
weigh, ranked by how often they came up against how much they mattered in
hindsight. Three lines of inquiry the script is built to surface:

1. **The herd.** How much of the decision was "people I respect are already using
   it." Everyone does this; nobody admits it unprompted. Q6 in the script is
   designed to make admitting it comfortable.
2. **Lock-in and the price you'll pay later.** What it would take to get out, and
   what the cost looked like a year in versus at signing. This is the sharpest
   business criterion in the set and it's nearly absent from the public
   conversation — which is exactly why owning it is worth something.
3. **The criteria they earned.** What they'd check *first* next time. This is the
   money question and the literal headline of the published piece.

**We never ask "which tool is best."** Tool names surface in the answers on their
own, which means we can report the landscape without having framed a contest.

## Shape

**Tier 1 — the public ask.** One question on LinkedIn (not X — wrong room for this
audience), live 5 days, answered through UpSight. Target **15–25 responses.**
Deliberately lower than v1: this audience doesn't dogpile a post, and a smaller,
right-shaped sample beats a big wrong-shaped one.

**Tier 2 — the interviews. This is the campaign.** **10–12** operators, 15 minutes
each, conversational UpSight interview. Recruited from:

- The Apollo consultant/fractional list already being built (Workstream 2) — the
  interview ask *is* the first touch.
- Warm local ecosystems already mapped in `40-gtm/outbound/targeting-and-response-playbook.md` §5:
  CONNECT members, EvoNexus portfolio founders, Rady Founders' Society.
- The four warm deals — Cytodyme, SSD, Patricia, Paul Jarrett. An interview request
  is a legitimate, non-pushy reason to re-open a stale thread.
- Snowball: the script's Q10 asks whose judgment they trust. Every name is a warm
  intro request.

## Why this is on-lane, not off it

v1 needed an ICP guardrail because it was off-lane. This doesn't. Fractional COOs
and CMOs are **segment A, rank 1** in the outbound playbook — the locked ICP. The
topic is a decision they are all actively making and none of them have a framework
for, which is the definition of a good wedge.

It also feeds two things already in flight:
- **Outbound template #3** (survey-respondent), called "the strongest play we have."
  This campaign generates its raw material from the exact right people.
- **Video assets F and G**, both blocked on capture debt. This is the shoot — and
  the footage is now UpSight interviewing a real buyer persona, not a bystander.

## Rick's own stake — keep it, it still works

v1's spine was "I have a real decision riding on this." Don't lose it. Rick *is*
this audience: a small-company operator choosing an AI stack, who also advises
clients on theirs. So the framing is peer, not researcher:

> *"I'm making this call for my own company and getting asked it by clients. I'd
> rather learn from people who've already made it than guess."*

True, non-salesy, and it's the reason a busy COO says yes.

## The publication

Headline shape: *"I asked 20 operators how they picked their AI stack. Here's what
they actually checked — and what they'd check first next time."*

1. The claim: everyone is making this call and almost nobody has criteria.
2. **The criteria set** — the 5–8 things that came up, ranked by frequency against
   how much they mattered in hindsight. This is the asset.
3. The gap: what people checked at signing vs. what actually bit them a year in.
   (Prediction worth testing: features at signing, lock-in and adoption a year in.)
4. The herd finding, stated without contempt — including how many said it out loud.
5. **The quote that changed my mind.** One. Named.
6. What I'm doing with it, for my own company.
7. One line of mechanism: *"every answer above is a receipt in UpSight — that
   clustering is the product."*

Named interviewees see it before it goes live. Non-negotiable.

## Kill criteria

- **<6 interview yeses by Wed 8/19** → the ask isn't landing. Stop, and diagnose
  whether it's the list or the pitch before spending week 2 on it.
- **<8 public responses at 5 days** → drop Tier 1 silently, publish on interviews
  alone. Ten named operators is a real piece; a thin public N is not a failure worth
  announcing.
- **Slips past 8/28** → publish what exists. Don't let it eat week 2.

## Risks

- **Reach is lower than v1.** Accepted and priced in. This trades reach for room
  quality: 12 conversations with buyers beats 300 impressions from engineers.
- **Busy execs no-show.** Real. Mitigation: 15 minutes, hard-stopped, their choice
  of voice/video/chat, and offer the full synthesis before publication — that last
  one is the honest reason a busy person says yes.
- **It reads as lead-gen in a trench coat.** It partly is, so the defense has to be
  real: publish the piece whether or not it produces meetings, quote people
  accurately, and send the synthesis to everyone who gave time. Do that and the
  motion is repeatable; skip it once and it's dead.
- **Turning into a listicle.** The criteria set is only worth something if it
  includes the uncomfortable findings — the herd behavior, the lock-in nobody
  checked. Cut the piece if it comes out flattering.
