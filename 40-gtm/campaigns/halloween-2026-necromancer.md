# The Necromancer — Halloween 2026

> Status: **captured idea, not yet built.** Task `902421b8` in UpSight Core, assets due Oct 5.
> Launch window Oct 20–31. Hard stop Nov 3.
> Sits on top of the "call graveyard" homepage positioning.

## The idea

Every team now records everything — Zoom, Gong, Granola, Fireflies. They pay for
all of it and learn nothing from any of it. Summaries land in a Slack channel
nobody reopens. It's a transcript graveyard, and they pay rent on every plot.

For one month, UpSight is the necromancer that raises them.

**Campaign line:** *"Your calls aren't dead. They're just buried."*

**Hero offer — "Exhume one call":** bring one old recording, get one full lens
read back, citations included. This is the existing 3-call activation wearing a
costume. **No product work required to run it.**

## The constraint that makes it work

The necromancer is a **campaign character, never a product claim** — and the
distinction is not squeamishness, it's the whole positioning.

UpSight's position is that it does **not** put words in anyone's mouth. Every
claim traces to the moment someone actually said it. A séance / medium / "we make
your calls speak" framing describes **fabrication** — precisely what the product
refuses to do, and what the engineering team spent a week removing from it.

So: on the evergreen site, UpSight is the **archaeologist**. Nothing is conjured.
Everything was already there, on the record, with a timestamp.

The necromancer is allowed to be the joke in October *precisely because the
product is the opposite of one.* That tension is the campaign. Lose it and the
campaign undermines the product.

## Emotional physics — get this right

**The dead thing is always the recording's value.** Never the customer, never the
rep, never the team. Bodies imply people; the thing in the ground here is a
transcript nobody could read.

The payoff is **relief, not dread**: nothing you recorded was wasted — nobody
could read it, and now something can.

## Do

- **Tombstones.** The one safe prop. A tombstone is text on a marker with a date,
  which is exactly what a call summary in Slack already is. That joke lands.
- **"Buried in March, answered in October"** — a series of real, anonymized
  examples of answers found in old calls. **Audit every one. No composite
  fiction.** The campaign about not making things up cannot make things up.
- **Teaser week of Oct 13** — *"What's buried in your Gong account?"*
- A **self-contained landing page** under `40-gtm/campaigns/`, plus social and
  email. It 301s to the homepage on Nov 3 and the metaphor goes back in the box.

## Do not

- **Skeletons or corpses.** Bodies imply people. The dead thing is value.
- **Séance, ghost, or medium imagery.** Fabrication-coded — anti-brand at the
  deepest level. This is the one that would actually hurt.
- **Horror-movie or fear-of-AI tones.** The competitor is indifference, not fear.
- **Discount codes in costume.**
- **Any cross-branding onto thedecisionfiles.com.** That property is
  evidence-solemn and sits the holiday out entirely.
- **A homepage takeover or site-wide skin.** The evergreen page uses "graveyard"
  exactly once, lowercase, as diagnosis. This campaign never touches it.

## Where the metaphor stops

Graveyard works as **diagnosis** — it names sunk cost (you paid for every plot)
and implies the answer is *present but buried*, which is the product claim
exactly. It breaks the moment it describes the **product** instead of the
**problem**.

For the evergreen homepage, non-morbid alternatives worth testing as the
diagnosis line:

- **Black box** — *"You have the flight recorder. Nobody's ever pulled the tape."*
- **No librarian** — *"You built a library and never hired a librarian."* Names
  the real defect: it's an indexing problem, not a recording one.
- **Cold storage** — *"You're paying rent on a storage unit you never open."*

Note the homepage headline itself — *"Your last 100 calls already answered this"* —
carries **no metaphor at all.** The metaphor is only load-bearing for October.
