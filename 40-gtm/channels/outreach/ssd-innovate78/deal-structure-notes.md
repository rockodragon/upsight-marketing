## The core structuring call

Three ways to shape this, one clear winner:

**A. Software-only sale.** SSD buys Team ($99/mo) + workspaces. **Rejected.** Rick's own words:
"even if they just bought my software, they wouldn't know what to do." The project fails, the
grant outcome goes unmet, and the failure is attached to our logo. Worst of all worlds.

**B. Staff augmentation (T&M).** Our data scientists billed hourly under SSD direction.
**Rejected.** SSD can't direct work they don't know how to scope — that's the whole problem.
T&M puts the management burden on the party least equipped to carry it, invites scope drift,
and prices us as bodies instead of outcomes.

**C. Fixed-fee, milestone-based research subcontract, powered by the UpSight platform.**
**← Recommended.** SSD stays prime (owns the Innovate78 relationship, community convening,
comms, credit). We deliver the research program end-to-end against defined milestones. The
platform is a separate line item that outlives the grant. Three reasons this is right:

1. **Grant-shaped.** Grant administrators want fixed deliverables tied to payments, not open
   hourly meters. Milestones map cleanly to grant reporting periods.
2. **SSD keeps a prime margin.** They need to retain program management + admin dollars to make
   this worth carrying. If we try to eat the whole $150K, the deal dies. Target subcontract:
   **$95–115K**, leaving SSD $35–55K (typical prime retention is 25–40% on pass-through work).
3. **The platform is the wedge to recurring revenue.** A consulting firm would deliver a PDF
   that's stale in six months. We deliver a *living* map in an SSD-owned UpSight workspace —
   and the renewal argument ("keep the map alive: $X/yr platform + an annual refresh
   engagement") writes itself. This converts a one-time grant into an annuity and makes SSD
   a flagship nonprofit/ecosystem case study.

## Why we win this (the pitch spine)

- **Evidence-linked, not adjective-linked.** Every theme in the report links to a founder's
  actual words. City councils get receipts, not consultant vibes. This is the product's core
  differentiator applied to a civic deliverable.
- **A living system, not a shelf PDF.** The map keeps updating; SSD can query it forever.
- **Local + already inside.** SSD has an active UpSight workspace; we're in their community.
  No discovery tax, no flyover consultants.
- **Price.** A national firm's discovery phase alone would eat the grant.

## The Erika objection = our design principle

Known blocker (pipeline.md): Erika's concern about uploading/emailing their lists — "maybe
they didn't give permission." Don't argue with it — **build the proposal around it.** Phase 0
includes a formal consent & data-governance audit as the *first deliverable*: what data SSD
holds, what permission basis exists, what can be used for research outreach vs. what needs
fresh opt-in. Data lives in an SSD-owned workspace; SSD/Innovate78 own it; we process it.
This turns the deal's biggest blocker into proof we're the careful choice.
