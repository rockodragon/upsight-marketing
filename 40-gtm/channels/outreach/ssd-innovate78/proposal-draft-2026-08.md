**A data collection & analysis program for Startup San Diego, in service of the Innovate78 initiative**

## 1. What Innovate78 has asked for, and why it's hard

Innovate78 — the partnership of Carlsbad, Escondido, Oceanside, San Marcos, and Vista, with
CSUSM and MiraCosta College — has funded Startup San Diego **[CONFIRM: $150,000, grant period]**
to map the entrepreneurial ecosystem of the 78 corridor: who the founders are, what support
exists, where the gaps are, and what the five cities should do about it.

This sounds like a directory project. It isn't. It's a data science project:

- The raw material is **scattered and messy** — email lists, event registrations, partner
  rosters, program alumni, LinkedIn, public business records. The same founder appears five
  times under three spellings; the same company appears as a startup in one list and a sponsor
  in another. Turning that into one trustworthy picture is an entity-resolution and analysis
  problem, not a spreadsheet exercise.
- The most valuable data **doesn't exist yet** — what founders actually experience along the
  corridor has to be collected through well-designed surveys and interviews, then analyzed
  rigorously enough that a city council can act on it.
- A map that's accurate in month one and stale by month six is a **wasted grant**. The
  deliverable has to be a living system someone can maintain.

Startup San Diego owns the community relationships and the convening power this project needs.
What we bring is the other half: a research team and a purpose-built customer-intelligence
platform to run the data work end-to-end.

## 3. Approach

### Phase 0 — Design & data governance (Months 1–2)

- Working sessions with SSD and Innovate78 stakeholders to fix the research questions, success
  metrics, and the deliverable each city expects.
- **Data inventory & consent audit** — the first formal deliverable. Every dataset SSD holds
  (email lists, event data, program records) is cataloged with its permission basis: what may
  be used for research outreach as-is, what requires fresh opt-in, what stays out. Nothing is
  uploaded or contacted before this audit clears it.
- Data governance charter: data lives in a Startup San Diego–owned workspace; Startup San
  Diego and Innovate78 own the data; UpSight processes it under agreement; PII handling and
  retention rules in writing.

### Phase 1 — Data collection (Months 2–5)

- **Consolidate what exists**: ingest and entity-resolve SSD's lists, partner rosters, and
  public/secondary sources (business registrations, funding databases, university program
  data) into a single people-and-organizations graph.
- **Founder & ecosystem survey**: a corridor-wide instrument (designed to research standards,
  not a Google Form) reaching founders, operators, and support organizations, distributed
  through SSD's and the cities' channels.
- **Stakeholder interviews**: recorded, transcribed conversations with founders, city economic
  development staff, investors, and university partners across all five cities
  **[CONFIRM: target 30–50 interviews]**. Every interview becomes searchable, quotable
  evidence — not notes in a drawer.

### Phase 2 — Analysis & synthesis (Months 4–7)

- Ecosystem census: startups, founders, and support assets by city, industry cluster, and stage.
- Thematic analysis of survey + interview data — every theme backed by linked, verbatim
  founder evidence, so findings carry receipts rather than anecdotes.
- Gap analysis: where founders fall through (capital, talent, space, connection), where they
  leave the corridor and why, what each city has versus what its founders use.

### Phase 3 — Deliverables & readouts (Months 7–9)

- **Regional report** — the state of the 78-corridor entrepreneurial ecosystem, with
  evidence-linked findings and prioritized recommendations.
- **Five city readouts** — each city's slice of the same dataset: its assets, its founders,
  its gaps.
- **The living ecosystem map** — an interactive, queryable map of people, organizations, and
  themes, hosted in Startup San Diego's workspace, not a static PDF.
- Stakeholder presentations to Innovate78 **[CONFIRM: and city councils as required]**.

### Phase 4 — Handoff & sustainability (Months 9–12)

- Training for SSD staff: running the map day-to-day, adding new founders and events,
  querying it for programming and sponsor conversations.
- A documented annual-refresh playbook, so year-two updates cost a fraction of year one.

## 7. Why UpSight

- **Receipts, not adjectives.** Our platform links every finding to the founder's actual
  words. When a recommendation reaches a city council, the evidence is one click away.
- **A living map, not a shelf report.** Consulting firms deliver PDFs that decay. The dataset
  we build stays queryable, updatable, and owned by Startup San Diego.
- **Already here.** Startup San Diego runs an active UpSight workspace today; we're part of
  this community, not a flyover firm — and the entire program costs less than a national
  consultancy's discovery phase.
- **Consent-first by design.** The permission audit is deliverable #1, because trust with this
  community's data is the project.
