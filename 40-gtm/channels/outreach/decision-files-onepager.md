## What it is

Independent research on how people actually decide.

One decision at a time: what the situation was, what was at stake, how they decided, and how it
actually turned out. Primary interviews. Published.

## Why it doesn't exist yet

- **Analyst firms** ask buyers with surveys — n=400, percentages.
- **Win/loss firms** ask properly, but privately. The vendor pays; nobody else sees it.
- **Everyone else** writes about vendors and products.

Nobody publishes real qualitative research on how buyers actually decide.

> Everyone counts buyers. Nobody asks them properly, in public.

## Who's behind it

Rick Moy. Founded and ran an empirical product-effectiveness testing firm from 2007 —
independent testing sold into the Fortune 500, nearly acquired by Gartner. Same principle here:
independence is the product.

## Every file carries one diagram

Not decoration. The diagram is the finding, readable in ten seconds. Eight patterns, matched to
the kind of decision — see the link above for worked examples.

The default is **what we said mattered vs. what actually mattered**: their own scorecard on the
left, what really decided it on the right. In the worked example, 65% of the scorecard weight
separated nobody, a line worth 10% made the call, and the two things that hurt most weren't on
the sheet at all.

Others: **who dropped out and why** · **a feature matrix with the row that actually decided it
highlighted** · **what the money actually bought** (cost split by the problem it solves, not
TCO) · **what got skipped** when there was no time.

**And every cell traces back to a quote.** Click it, hear the person say it.

## The rules

- A real person, a real date, real stakes, direct quotes, and a real outcome — or it doesn't publish.
- **Named, role-only ("a CTO at a 200-person fintech"), or anonymous.** Your call.
- You approve every quote before it runs.
- No vendor sees a file before publication. No sponsor touches subject selection, questions, or findings.
- Plain English throughout. If a label needs a glossary, it's the wrong label.

## What we'd ask of you

15 minutes, hard-stopped. That's it.

Later, if it's useful to you: a couple of introductions a quarter, and a read before we publish.
