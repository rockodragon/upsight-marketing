# Delegated Surveys: Community Research Through the People Residents Already Trust

**A delegated survey is one survey, distributed through many trusted collectors, where every response still lands in one place with the collector's name attached.** The organizer writes the questions once. Table hosts, block captains, chapter leads or case managers each get their own link. Residents answer the person they already know. The organizer sees who has been heard from, who is missing, and which collector reached them.

Community research rarely fails on question design. It fails on distribution. The people you most need to hear from do not open a survey link from an agency they have never met. They do open one from their pastor, their block captain, their kid's coach, or the neighbor who hosts the table at the fundraiser. Today those people collect on paper, in a group text, or in a spreadsheet they email you on Friday night. You lose attribution, you cannot see coverage while collection is still open, and you re-key everything.

Delegated surveys put the structure under that reality instead of fighting it.

## What is a delegated survey?

Three parts, one dataset.

1. **One survey.** The organizer owns the questions, the identity rules (name and email, phone, or anonymous), the languages, and the deadline.
2. **Many collectors.** Each table host, cohort lead or group lead gets a personal link and a private console. No account, no training beyond two lines of instructions.
3. **Two ways to collect.** A collector can share their link or QR code so residents answer themselves, or enter a response on someone's behalf after a phone call, a doorstep conversation or a paper form.

The resident stays the respondent. The record belongs to them, not to the collector. The collector's name and the collection mode travel with each response, so "Maria, Block 4, entered by host" is a fact in the data rather than a note in your head.

## Why does distribution through trusted people change the data you get?

Consider what a single public link produces. After the Los Angeles fires, the State of California ran an online process asking survivors what recovery should prioritize. It gathered 1,289 comments and sorted them into 11 topics and 38 groups. Of those comments, 276 landed in "Other" and 105 got no group at all. Roughly 30 percent of what residents wrote never became a theme anyone acted on. The state did real work here and published the data, which is more than most programs do. A single link hears from whoever finds it, and sorts what it hears into whatever buckets were drawn in advance.

Delegated collection changes both halves.

- **Reach the people a link misses.** The ask comes from someone the resident already answers. The collector can also sit with them and type.
- **See coverage while collection is open.** Completion by collector, against the capacity you set, shows which block or chapter is quiet before the deadline, not after.
- **No re-keying.** A response a host typed in lands in the same dataset as one a resident submitted. The export carries the collector and the mode as columns.
- **Every answer is evidence attached to a person.** Themes link to the exact words and to who said them, so the follow-up goes to the right household and the report can show its sources.
- **Say exactly how the sample was built.** You know which collectors reached whom. A reader can judge the sample because you can describe it.

## How do you run one?

1. **Write one short survey.** Ten questions or fewer. Decide whether residents identify themselves or stay anonymous, and translate questions for the languages your community speaks. UpSight surveys run in form, chat or voice mode, so a collector at a kitchen table can use whichever fits.
2. **Name your collectors and their capacity.** "Jane Smith, Table 4, 10 seats." "Pastor Ruiz, Thursday group, 25 people." Capacity is what turns a count into a coverage number.
3. **Send each collector their link and two lines of instructions.** Option A: forward the link or show the QR code. Option B: open your console and enter people yourself. Include the deadline and who to call for help.
4. **Watch coverage by collector, not the total.** A total of 400 responses hides that one neighborhood produced 350 of them. Turn links off when a group is done or when a collector drops out.
5. **Analyze with the receipts intact.** Group the answers into themes, keep every quote linked to the response and the person behind it, and export with the collector and mode columns when a partner wants the raw table.
6. **Close the loop.** Tell collectors and residents what was heard and what happens next. The outside review of the California program said participants need to see how their input was used. A collector who can show their group that the answers went somewhere will collect for you again.

## Where does this fit?

- **Event tables and sponsor blocks.** The organizer sells the table; the host owns the guest list. Delegated intake gets names, contacts and meal preferences without the host learning a new tool. This is the case the feature was built for.
- **Neighborhood recovery and resilience planning.** Block captains and mutual-aid leads collect from households that will never attend a town hall.
- **Membership organizations with chapters.** Each chapter lead runs the same survey; the national office sees completion by chapter.
- **Engagement consultancies.** A general plan or housing element appendix needs public input that can be traced back. Community partners become collectors, and the appendix can state who reached whom.
- **Schools, youth and workforce programs.** Coaches, cohort leads and case managers already have the relationship. Give them the link.

## What a delegated survey is not

It is not a representative sample by default. It is a sample you can describe honestly, because the collector and mode are recorded for every response. State that in the report.

It is not a substitute for conversation. It is the structured layer under it. When an answer deserves a longer story, follow up with that person in chat or voice mode. The response already tells you who they are and who introduced them.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a delegated survey?**
One survey distributed through many trusted collectors, such as table hosts or group leads, where every response is attributed to the collector who reached that person and stored in one dataset.

**How is this different from posting a survey link in a group chat?**
A shared link tells you nothing about who reached whom. A delegated link records the collector and whether the resident answered themselves or the collector entered it, so you can see coverage by group while collection is open.

**Can a collector see other people's responses?**
No. A collector's console shows only the people in their own group. Full results and analysis stay with the organizer.

**Do residents need an account?**
No. They open the collector's link or scan the QR code and answer. Identity is whatever the organizer chose: name and email, phone, or anonymous.

**Can a collector enter answers for someone?**
Yes. Responses entered by a collector are marked as delegate entry and still belong to the resident, who can complete missing fields later from their own link.

**Does UpSight support delegated surveys?**
