# What is thematic analysis?

**Thematic analysis is the method of reading qualitative data — interviews, open-text survey answers, call transcripts — tagging recurring ideas, and grouping those tags into themes that describe what the whole body of conversations is actually about.**

The traditional process is: familiarize yourself with the data, generate initial codes, group codes into candidate themes, review and merge them, name them, and write up with supporting quotes. AI tooling now automates the first three steps well and the last two badly, because naming a theme is an act of judgment about what matters.

## Why it matters

Without it, qualitative research collapses into whichever quotes the analyst remembers. Thematic analysis is the difference between "several people mentioned onboarding" and a defensible count of how many, who, and in what terms.

## Example

Across 22 interviews, 47 coded excerpts cluster into five themes. The largest — 14 excerpts from 11 people — is initially named "reporting problems," but reading the quotes shows it is really about *explaining results to someone else*. The rename changes the roadmap implication entirely.

## FAQ

**What is thematic analysis in simple terms?**

Reading everything customers said, marking the ideas that keep coming up, and grouping them into a short list of what the research is about.

**Can AI do thematic analysis?**

AI is strong at coding and clustering excerpts and weak at judging which theme matters. Treat the output as a first pass to be reviewed against the quotes.

**How many themes should emerge?**

Most studies land on four to seven meaningful themes. Twenty themes usually means the codes were never merged.
