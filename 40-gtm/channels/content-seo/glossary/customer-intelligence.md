# What is customer intelligence?

**Customer intelligence is the practice of turning every customer conversation — calls, surveys, interviews, and notes — into a durable, evidence-backed understanding of who your customers are, what they need, and why it matters.**

It differs from customer feedback in one important way. Feedback is a collection of individual signals: a survey response, a support ticket, a line in a call transcript. Customer intelligence is what you know after those signals have been connected to the people who said them and to each other. Feedback answers *what was said*. Customer intelligence answers *what we now know, and who told us*.

## Why it matters

Most companies collect far more feedback than they can use. The notetaker remembers one meeting. The survey tool remembers one response. The research repository remembers what someone tagged. None of them remember the customer. The result is teams asking the same customer the same question twice, and decisions made on the loudest recent signal rather than the accumulated evidence.

## Example

A team runs 18 discovery interviews over a quarter. Feedback gives them 18 transcripts. Customer intelligence gives them: 6 recurring themes, the 11 people who raised each one, the exact quotes behind every theme, and the knowledge that 4 of those people are in the same segment — which changes whether the theme is a market pattern or one company's problem.

## FAQ

**What is customer intelligence in simple terms?**

It is knowing your customers, not just storing their feedback. The difference is whether your understanding survives past the individual meeting or survey it came from.

**How is customer intelligence different from market research?**

Market research studies a population to answer a defined question and then ends. Customer intelligence is continuous and person-level: it accumulates against named individuals and relationships over time.

**Who uses customer intelligence?**

Product teams deciding what to build, leadership deciding where to invest, and marketing deciding what to say — ideally from the same evidence base rather than three separate ones.
