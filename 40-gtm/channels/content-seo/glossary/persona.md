# What is a persona?

**A persona is a research-based composite of a specific type of user or buyer — their goals, constraints, workflow, and the conditions under which they act — used so that teams can reason about a consistent person rather than an abstraction.**

Personas earned a bad reputation because most are invented rather than researched: a stock photo, a fictional name, an age, a coffee preference, and no evidence. A persona is only as good as the interviews behind it, and the demographic details are almost never the useful part.

## Why it matters

The useful content of a persona is behavioral and situational: what triggers them to look for a solution, what they've already tried, who they need permission from, and what would make them abandon the effort. Those change how you build and how you sell. Age and job title rarely do.

## Example

"Marketing Mary, 34, likes yoga" tells a team nothing. "Evaluates tools between January planning and the March board meeting, has three failed rollouts behind her, and needs something the team will adopt without training" tells them what to build and what to say.

## FAQ

**How many personas should you have?**

As few as possible — usually one primary, sometimes a secondary. Teams with six personas generally serve none of them well.

**What makes a persona useless?**

Being invented, being demographic rather than behavioral, and never being updated after the workshop that produced it.

**What's the difference between a persona and a JTBD?**

A persona describes who someone is; a job describes the progress they want in a situation. The same person hires different products for different jobs.
