# What is a customer 360 view?

**A customer 360 is a single unified view of everything an organization knows about one customer — transactions, support history, product usage, and conversations — assembled from systems that would otherwise each hold a fragment.**

In practice most customer 360 projects unify *structured* data: fields, events, and transactions. The qualitative half — what the customer actually said in calls, interviews, and open-text responses — is the part that usually stays outside the view, because it does not fit a field.

## Why it matters

A 360 view made only of structured data can tell you a customer's usage dropped 40% last month. It cannot tell you that they said on a call in March that their champion was leaving. The second fact explains the first, and it is the one that is missing.

## Example

Two accounts show identical usage decline. One said "we're heads-down on an audit until June." The other said "we couldn't get the team to switch over." Same data, opposite responses required, and the difference is only in the conversation record.

## In UpSight

UpSight covers the conversational half of the 360 — calls, surveys, interviews, and notes attached to a persistent person-level profile — and is normally used alongside, not instead of, the systems holding transactional data.

## FAQ

**What data goes into a customer 360?**

Typically CRM records, billing, support tickets, product usage, and marketing engagement — plus, if done well, the qualitative record of what the customer has said.

**Why do customer 360 projects fail?**

They are usually scoped as data integration projects and succeed at integration while never answering a question anyone was actually asking.

**Is customer 360 the same as customer intelligence?**

No. A 360 is a view of stored data. Customer intelligence is the understanding derived from it, especially from the conversational part.
