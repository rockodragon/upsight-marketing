# What is continuous discovery?

**Continuous discovery is the habit of talking to customers on a regular weekly cadence, in small doses, so that product decisions are always informed by recent evidence rather than by a research study that ended months ago.**

The idea is most associated with Teresa Torres's *Continuous Discovery Habits*, which proposes a working definition: at minimum, weekly touchpoints with customers by the team building the product, in pursuit of a desired outcome. The emphasis on *the team* is deliberate — outsourced research does not produce the same decision-making.

## Why it matters

Project-based research goes stale on a predictable schedule. A study finished in March is being used to argue about a roadmap in September, by which point the market, the competitors, and the customers have all moved. A weekly cadence means the evidence is never more than a few days old.

## Example

A team doing one interview per week accumulates roughly 50 conversations a year with no single week costing more than an hour. A team doing quarterly research sprints does maybe 40, in four bursts, and spends the intervening weeks arguing from the last one.

## In UpSight

Continuity is the point of the platform: weekly conversations compound into person-level profiles and themes rather than resetting with each study.

## FAQ

**How often is 'continuous'?**

The common benchmark is at least one customer conversation per week, conducted by the people who build the product.

**Isn't weekly interviewing too slow to be useful?**

The compounding is the point. Fifty conversations a year with running synthesis beats two large studies, because the learning arrives while decisions are still open.

**Who should run the interviews?**

The product trio — product, design, engineering — or in a small company, the founder. Handing it entirely to a research team separates the learning from the deciding.
