# What is the difference between qualitative and quantitative research?

**Qualitative research explains *why* something happens and *what* exists in a population — through interviews, open text, and observation. Quantitative research measures *how much* and *how many* — through surveys with designed samples, experiments, and analytics.**

The two are sequential more often than they are alternatives. Qualitative work generates the hypotheses and supplies the language; quantitative work sizes them. Running a survey before any interviews usually means writing answer options that don't match how customers actually describe the problem.

## Why it matters

Teams mistake one for the other constantly. A 30-response open-text survey is qualitative data with a misleading percentage attached to it. Reporting "40% said X" from 30 self-selected responses gives a qualitative finding the false authority of a statistic.

## Example

Interviews reveal that people distrust an automated number. That's qualitative: it tells you the concern exists and what it sounds like. A survey then tells you 62% of 400 respondents share it. You needed the first to know to ask the second.

## FAQ

**Which comes first, qualitative or quantitative?**

Qualitative, almost always. It tells you what to measure and in what words to ask about it.

**Can you get percentages from interviews?**

You can count, but you cannot generalize. Say "7 of 19 interviewees" rather than "37% of customers" — the first is honest, the second is not.

**Is open-text survey data qualitative?**

Yes. The delivery mechanism is a survey but the data requires qualitative analysis.
