# What is an interview guide?

**An interview guide is the written plan for a research conversation: the objectives, the opening, the main question sequence, the probes to use when an answer is thin, and the close.**

A guide is not a script. It exists so that ten interviews cover comparable ground and so the interviewer can follow an unexpected thread without losing the thread they came for. Good guides are short — often a single page — and front-load the questions that matter most, because time always runs out.

## Why it matters

Without a guide, each interview drifts toward whatever the participant found most interesting, and the set cannot be compared. With a script read verbatim, the participant never volunteers the thing you didn't know to ask. The guide is the middle position.

## Example

A one-page guide: 2 minutes of context, 15 minutes reconstructing the last time the problem occurred, 10 minutes on what they tried and what it cost, 5 minutes on who else is involved in deciding, 3 minutes for anything you should have asked.

## FAQ

**How long should an interview guide be?**

One page. Guides longer than that are scripts in disguise and get read rather than used.

**Should you send the guide to participants in advance?**

Usually not — prepared answers are less useful than spontaneous ones. Sending the topic and the time commitment is enough.

**What's the most important question in any guide?**

The last one: "what should I have asked you that I didn't?" It routinely produces the best material in the session.
