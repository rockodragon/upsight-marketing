# What is product-market fit?

**Product-market fit is the state in which a well-defined market segment reliably buys, adopts, and continues to use your product because it solves a problem they were already actively trying to solve.**

It is a property of a product-and-segment pair, not of a product. The same product can have fit with solo consultants and none with enterprise teams. Claims of "we have PMF" that don't name the segment are usually describing a handful of enthusiastic early customers.

## Why it matters

Almost every scaling failure is a company adding sales capacity before fit exists in a defined segment. The signals are behavioral: retention flattening rather than decaying, usage without prompting, unprompted referrals, and customers describing the product to each other in your own words.

## Example

The most-cited survey measure asks existing users how they would feel if they could no longer use the product, with the commonly quoted benchmark being 40% or more answering "very disappointed." It is a useful signal, not a certificate — the composition of who you surveyed matters as much as the number.

## FAQ

**How do you know if you have product-market fit?**

Retention flattens instead of decaying, usage continues without prompting, and customers refer others unprompted — within one named segment.

**What is the 40% rule for product-market fit?**

A commonly used survey benchmark: at least 40% of users saying they would be 'very disappointed' without the product. Treat it as one signal among several.

**Can you lose product-market fit?**

Yes. Markets, competitors, and buyer expectations move, which is the argument for continuous discovery rather than a one-time validation.
