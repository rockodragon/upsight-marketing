# What is assumption testing?

**Assumption testing is the practice of writing down what must be true for an idea to work, ranking those beliefs by how risky and how unknown they are, and designing the cheapest possible test for the riskiest one.**

Assumptions usually fall into categories: desirability (do they want it), viability (does it make money), feasibility (can we build it), and usability (can they operate it). The valuable ones sit in the top-right quadrant of an assumption map: high impact if wrong, and currently unevidenced.

## Why it matters

Teams test what is easy to test, which is usually feasibility, because engineers can answer it. The assumption that actually kills products is almost always desirability, and it is answerable in a week of conversations for a fraction of the cost of the build.

## Example

Assumption: "consultants will pay for this out of their own pocket." Cheapest test is not a build — it's asking six consultants what they currently pay for out of pocket, and for what. Two hours, and it can end the project.

## FAQ

**What is the riskiest assumption test?**

The cheapest experiment that could disprove the belief most likely to kill the idea. Cheapest and most-fatal, in that order.

**How do you prioritize assumptions?**

On two axes: how damaging it would be if the assumption is wrong, and how much evidence you currently have. Test high-damage, low-evidence first.

**Can you test assumptions without building anything?**

Usually yes. Conversations, landing pages, concierge delivery, and manual prototypes answer most desirability assumptions.
