# What is ResearchOps?

**ResearchOps is the operational layer that makes customer research repeatable: participant recruiting, scheduling, consent and privacy handling, incentives, data storage, and who is allowed to see what.**

It is the unglamorous half of research and the half that determines throughput. Teams rarely stop doing discovery because they ran out of questions; they stop because recruiting takes three weeks, the consent form is unresolved, and nobody knows where last quarter's recordings went.

## Why it matters

Every hour of ResearchOps friction is subtracted directly from research volume. For a small team, the highest-leverage ops investment is usually a standing recruiting source and a default consent process — those two remove most of the delay between deciding to talk to someone and talking to them.

## Example

A founder who keeps a warm list of 30 people who have agreed to be contacted for research can run an interview within 48 hours of a question arising. Without the list, the same question waits two weeks and often gets decided without evidence.

## FAQ

**Do small teams need ResearchOps?**

They need the function, not the role. A solo founder doing it well means a recruiting list, a consent line, and a consistent place recordings land.

**What slows research down the most?**

Recruiting, by a wide margin. It is the first thing worth systematizing.

**What are the privacy obligations in research?**

At minimum: clear consent for recording and use, a stated retention period, and a way for participants to withdraw. Requirements vary by jurisdiction — confirm what applies to your participants.
