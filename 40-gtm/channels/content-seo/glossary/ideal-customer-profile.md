# What is an ideal customer profile (ICP)?

**An ideal customer profile (ICP) describes the type of *company* that gets the most value from your product, is cheapest to acquire, and is most likely to stay — a firmographic and situational filter, not a description of a person.**

A usable ICP is specific enough to disqualify. "B2B SaaS companies" is not an ICP. "Series A B2B SaaS companies with a founder still running sales and no dedicated research function" is, because you can look at a company and say no.

## Why it matters

Without a disqualifying ICP, every inbound lead looks equally worth pursuing and the roadmap gets pulled toward whoever complained most recently. The ICP is as much a tool for saying no as for targeting.

## Example

A team's best 10 accounts share three traits the other 40 don't: a founder who still takes sales calls, an existing survey habit, and fewer than 50 employees. That intersection is the ICP, and it was derived from the customer base rather than invented in a planning session.

## FAQ

**What's the difference between an ICP and a persona?**

An ICP describes the company. A persona describes a person inside it. You need both and they answer different questions.

**How do you build an ICP?**

Look at your best existing accounts — by retention and expansion, not logo size — and find what they share that your worst accounts don't.

**Can you have more than one ICP?**

Early on, no. Multiple ICPs before product-market fit usually means the segment is not yet understood.
