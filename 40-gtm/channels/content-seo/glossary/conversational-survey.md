# What is a conversational survey?

**A conversational survey asks questions one at a time and generates follow-up probes based on what the respondent actually said — producing something closer to interview depth at survey scale.**

A traditional survey is fixed: every respondent sees the same questions in the same order, and an interesting open-text answer goes unexplored because there is nobody there to ask "why?". A conversational survey closes that gap by probing on the answer, the way an interviewer would.

## Why it matters

The two research modes have always traded off depth against reach. Interviews go deep with 20 people; surveys reach 500 shallowly. The interesting part of most survey datasets is the open text, and it is precisely the part that has no follow-up. Probing changes the economics of that trade-off.

## Example

Respondent writes "the reporting is frustrating." A fixed survey records five words. A conversational survey asks what they were trying to do, and gets the workflow, the workaround, and the reason — the material an interview would have produced.

## FAQ

**How is a conversational survey different from a chatbot?**

A chatbot is usually there to route or support. A conversational survey is a research instrument with defined objectives and analyzable output.

**Do conversational surveys have lower completion rates?**

They ask for more effort per question but often hold attention better than a long grid of fixed questions. Test it against your own audience rather than assuming.

**Can AI follow-ups introduce bias?**

Yes — a poorly prompted follow-up can lead the respondent exactly like a human interviewer can. Review generated probes for leading language.
