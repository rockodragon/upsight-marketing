# What is a discovery interview?

**A discovery interview is a semi-structured conversation designed to understand a person's real behavior, context, and constraints — what they did, when, and why — rather than to pitch them or collect opinions about an idea.**

The structure is usually: establish context, walk through a specific recent instance of the problem, probe what they tried and what it cost them, and only then, if at all, touch on what you are building. Thirty to forty-five minutes is typical. The interviewer should be talking for well under a third of it.

## Why it matters

Interviews that collect opinions produce agreeable, useless data — people are poor predictors of their own future behavior and are socially motivated to encourage you. Interviews that reconstruct a specific past episode produce facts, which are checkable.

## Example

"Would you use a tool that automated this?" reliably returns yes and means nothing. "Walk me through the last time this happened — what did you do first?" returns a workflow, a workaround, a cost, and often the name of a competitor you didn't know about.

## FAQ

**How long should a discovery interview be?**

Thirty to forty-five minutes. Past an hour, quality drops for both sides and scheduling gets much harder.

**What questions should you ask in a discovery interview?**

Past-tense, episode-specific questions: when did this last happen, what did you do, what did you try before, what did it cost you, who else was involved.

**Should you demo your product in a discovery interview?**

Not until the end, if at all. Demoing converts the conversation from research into a sales call and contaminates everything said afterward.
