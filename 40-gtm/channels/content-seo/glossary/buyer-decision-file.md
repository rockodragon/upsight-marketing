# What is a buyer decision file?

**A buyer decision file is a published account of how one real buying team actually evaluated a category — who was in the room, what criteria they applied, which options they eliminated, and the reason each one was eliminated.**

It differs from an analyst quadrant in what it claims. A quadrant ranks vendors against the analyst's criteria. A decision file reports one buyer's criteria, in the buyer's own words, with the evidence attached. It does not say which product is best; it says how this decision was actually made.

## Why it matters

Buyers evaluating a category want to know how people like them decided, not how an analyst scored features. The reasons that actually eliminate vendors — "too expensive for what it is," "our security team wouldn't sign off," "the champion left" — never appear in a feature matrix, and they are the deciding factors.

## Example

A file on AI tooling selection at a 400-person company reports: five vendors considered, two eliminated on procurement grounds before any demo, one on data residency, and the final choice made on which vendor's onboarding required the least change from the team's existing habits. None of that is a feature comparison.

## FAQ

**How is a decision file different from a case study?**

A case study is published by the vendor about a win. A decision file is published by a neutral party about a decision, including the vendors that lost and why.

**Who reads buyer decision files?**

Buyers running the same evaluation, and vendors who want to know why they are being eliminated before the shortlist.

**What makes a decision file credible?**

Named criteria in the buyer's own language, attributable evidence behind each claim, and a stated conflicts policy covering who paid for what.
