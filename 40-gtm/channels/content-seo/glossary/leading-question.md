# What is a leading question?

**A leading question is one that suggests its own answer, prompting agreement rather than producing information — the most common and most damaging error in customer research.**

Leading questions come in several forms: the loaded premise ("how frustrating is this process?"), the binary that hides the real range ("do you prefer A or B?" when the answer is neither), the double-barrelled question that asks two things at once, and the hypothetical ("would you use...?") which measures politeness rather than behavior.

## Why it matters

A leading question doesn't just fail to produce data — it produces false confidence. The team leaves with quotes that appear to validate the plan, which are actually echoes of the plan. That is worse than no research.

## Example

Leading: "How much time do you waste on manual reporting?" Presumes waste, manual, and reporting as a category. Neutral: "Walk me through how last month's report got made." The second one lets them tell you it takes four minutes and nobody minds.

## FAQ

**What is an example of a leading question?**

"Don't you think the current process is too slow?" It supplies the opinion and invites agreement.

**How do you avoid leading questions?**

Ask about specific past events rather than opinions or hypotheticals, remove adjectives from the question, and never name the answer you're hoping for.

**Are leading questions ever acceptable?**

In a solution interview you may legitimately present a concept and ask for reaction. That is different from embedding the conclusion in a problem-space question.
