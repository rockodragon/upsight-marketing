# What is thematic saturation?

**Thematic saturation is the point in qualitative research at which additional interviews stop producing new themes — the practical signal that you have enough data within that segment.**

Saturation is segment-specific and question-specific. Reaching it among solo founders says nothing about whether you have saturated among 200-person companies, and saturating on "how they work today" says nothing about "how they buy." Widening the question or the segment resets the clock.

## Why it matters

It is the only principled stopping rule qualitative research has. Without it, teams either stop arbitrarily when the calendar runs out or keep interviewing because more feels safer, and both produce worse decisions than a saturation check would.

## Example

Interviews 13, 14, and 15 all raise themes already present in the first twelve. That's saturation for this question and this segment. Interview 16 with a different segment produces two new themes immediately — confirming the saturation was local, exactly as expected.

## In UpSight

Running theme counts across a study make saturation observable while it's happening, rather than discovered afterward in analysis.

## FAQ

**How do you know you've reached saturation?**

Two or three consecutive interviews produce no theme you haven't already recorded, within the same segment and research question.

**Is saturation a rigorous standard?**

It is the accepted convention in qualitative research, but it depends on how the analysis is coded. Sloppy coding reaches 'saturation' early and falsely.

**Does saturation mean you can stop talking to customers?**

Only for that question. Continuous discovery keeps running because the questions keep changing.
