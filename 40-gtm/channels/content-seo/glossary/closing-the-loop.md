# What is closing the loop?

**Closing the loop means returning to the specific customer who gave you feedback and telling them what you did about it — shipped it, decided against it, or need more information.**

It is the final step of a feedback program and the one most often skipped, because it requires knowing which named person said what, months after they said it. Programs that aggregate feedback into anonymous counts structurally cannot close the loop; they no longer know who to go back to.

## Why it matters

Closing the loop is the highest-return follow-up available to most companies. It costs one email, it proves you were listening, it reliably produces a second round of more candid feedback, and "we built this because you asked" is the strongest expansion and referral message there is. Even a *no* closes well when the reasoning is shared.

## Example

Four customers asked for CSV export in Q1. Shipping it in Q2 and emailing those four specific people — quoting what each of them originally said — converts a feature release into four relationship moments. The same release announced only in a changelog produces none.

## In UpSight

Because evidence stays attached to the person who gave it, the list of who to go back to when something ships is a query rather than an archaeology project.

## FAQ

**Why do companies skip closing the loop?**

Usually not by choice — their tooling anonymized or aggregated the feedback, so they no longer know which individual to contact.

**What if we decided not to build what they asked for?**

Say so, with the reasoning. Customers respond well to a clear no and badly to silence.

**How long after shipping should you close the loop?**

Within days. The connection to what they asked for weakens quickly.
