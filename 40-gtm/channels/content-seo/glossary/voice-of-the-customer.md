# What is voice of the customer (VoC)?

**Voice of the customer (VoC) is the structured practice of capturing what customers say about their needs, expectations, and experience — in their own words — and routing it into the decisions that affect them.**

A VoC program typically draws on surveys, interviews, support conversations, sales calls, and reviews. The defining discipline is preserving the customer's own language rather than the internal paraphrase of it. "Too expensive" and "eliminated on cost criteria" describe the same event; only one of them is the voice of the customer.

## Why it matters

Paraphrase is where meaning leaks. Once a customer's words have been summarized into a category, the specific thing they were worried about is gone, and the decision made downstream is made on the summary. Programs that keep verbatim quotes attached to every finding are dramatically harder to argue with in a roadmap meeting.

## Example

A VoC program that reports "23% of respondents cited pricing concerns" is weaker than one that reports the same number alongside four verbatim quotes, because the quotes reveal that three of the four were not about price at all — they were about not being able to predict the bill.

## FAQ

**What is VoC in simple terms?**

Systematically listening to customers in their own words, and making sure what they said actually reaches the people making decisions.

**What are the main VoC methods?**

Surveys, one-on-one interviews, sales and support call analysis, reviews and community monitoring, and in-product feedback. Most programs combine at least three.

**What's the most common VoC mistake?**

Collecting continuously and synthesizing rarely. An unread VoC archive is a cost, not an asset.
