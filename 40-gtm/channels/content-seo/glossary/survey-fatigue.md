# What is survey fatigue?

**Survey fatigue is the decline in both response rate and answer quality that results from asking people too often, for too long, or with no visible consequence to their previous answers.**

It shows up first as falling completion rates, then as straight-lining — identical scores down a column — and finally as shorter open-text answers. The last symptom is the costly one, because open text is where the usable material is.

## Why it matters

Fatigue is usually diagnosed as a design problem and treated by shortening the survey. Length matters, but the bigger driver is consequence: people answer when they believe the answer goes somewhere. A program that never closes the loop trains its audience not to bother.

## Example

An NPS survey sent quarterly with no follow-up gets 12% response by year two. The same audience, asked one specific question after a support interaction and then told what changed, responds at multiples of that.

## In UpSight

Because responses stay attached to the person, going back to tell them what changed is a query rather than a project — which is the actual remedy for fatigue.

## FAQ

**How long should a survey be?**

Short enough to state the time cost honestly and have people believe it. Under five minutes for a broad audience; longer is possible with an engaged one.

**How often can you survey the same people?**

Depends entirely on whether they see results. Quarterly with visible follow-through beats monthly into silence.

**What is straight-lining?**

Selecting the same answer down every row to finish quickly. It is a reliable indicator that the responses in that block are worthless.
