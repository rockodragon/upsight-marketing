# How many customer interviews do you need?

**For qualitative discovery, most studies begin repeating themselves between 12 and 20 interviews within a single well-defined segment — and the number that matters is the count per segment, not the total across all of them.**

This is a practical heuristic rather than a statistical rule. Qualitative research does not produce statistically representative findings at any sample size; it produces an inventory of what exists in the population. The stopping condition is thematic saturation — when new interviews stop producing new themes.

## Why it matters

Teams get this wrong in both directions. Some stop at three conversations and treat one person's workflow as a market truth. Others delay acting until they have 60 interviews spread across five segments, which is 12 per segment — the same evidence they'd have had months earlier with a narrower scope.

## Example

Twenty interviews across four segments is five per segment: too thin to distinguish a pattern from a personality in any of them. Twenty in one segment will usually saturate. Narrowing the segment is almost always the better move.

## FAQ

**Is 5 customer interviews enough?**

Five is enough to find obvious usability problems in a defined task. It is not enough to establish that a market problem exists.

**How many interviews for statistical significance?**

None. Qualitative interviews are not a statistical instrument; if you need significance you need a survey with a designed sample.

**When should you stop interviewing?**

When two or three consecutive interviews in the same segment produce no new themes.
