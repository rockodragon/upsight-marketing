# What are evaluation criteria?

**Evaluation criteria are the standards a buying team uses to compare options. Every evaluation has two sets: the stated criteria that appear in the RFP or scorecard, and the unstated ones that do most of the actual eliminating.**

Stated criteria tend to be feature-shaped and are often written after the team already has a preference. Unstated criteria are things like whether the vendor felt responsive, whether anyone on the team has used it before, and how much internal change adoption would require. The second set is rarely written down and frequently decisive.

## Why it matters

Sellers who optimize only against stated criteria compete on a scorecard the buyer may have written to justify a decision already forming. Research that surfaces the unstated criteria explains losses that make no sense on the matrix.

## Example

A published scorecard weights integrations at 30%. The interview reveals the shortlist was actually set by which vendors two team members had used at a previous job. The integration score decided nothing.

## In UpSight

## FAQ

**What are typical B2B evaluation criteria?**

Functionality, price, security and compliance, integrations, support, implementation effort, and vendor viability — plus the unstated ones around familiarity and risk.

**How do you find a buyer's real criteria?**

Ask what eliminated the options that didn't make the shortlist. Elimination reasons are far more honest than selection reasons.

**Do criteria change during an evaluation?**

Frequently. Criteria discovered mid-evaluation, often from a demo, commonly override the original list.
