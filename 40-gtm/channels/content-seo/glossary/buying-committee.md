# What is a buying committee?

**A buying committee is the group of people who collectively decide a B2B purchase — typically an economic buyer, a champion, the end users, and functions such as security, legal, or procurement that can block but not approve.**

The important asymmetry is that most members can only say no. A champion advocates, an economic buyer approves, and security, legal, procurement, and IT each hold a veto they can exercise without ever attending a demo. Deals are lost by people the seller never met far more often than sellers assume.

## Why it matters

Discovery aimed only at the end user misses the constraints that actually decide the outcome. Asking "who else has to agree to this, and what would make them say no?" early in an evaluation surfaces vetoes while there is still time to address them.

## Example

A team sells to research managers and wins the user every time. Twelve losses later, the pattern is clear: IT vetoes on data residency in nine of them. The buying committee had a member the sales process never engaged.

## FAQ

**How many people are on a typical B2B buying committee?**

It varies widely by deal size, but any meaningful purchase usually involves more people than the seller has spoken to.

**Who is the most important person on a buying committee?**

The champion, because they sell internally when you are not in the room. But the veto holders determine whether that selling can succeed.

**How do you map a buying committee?**

Ask directly: who else needs to agree, what will they care about, and what has stopped a purchase like this before.
