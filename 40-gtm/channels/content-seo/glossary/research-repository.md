# What is a research repository?

**A research repository is a central store of customer research — transcripts, notes, clips, tags, and findings — organized so that past studies can be found and reused instead of repeated.**

Repositories solved a real problem: research used to disappear into individual researchers' folders. They introduced a different one. Because repositories organize around studies and tags, their value depends entirely on someone maintaining the tagging taxonomy, and that maintenance is the first thing dropped when the team gets busy.

## Why it matters

The honest test of a repository is whether anyone searches it who did not put something into it. Many are write-only: diligently filled, never read. Knowing which kind you have should drive whether you invest in taxonomy or in a different organizing principle altogether.

## Example

A team with 200 tagged sessions cannot answer "what has Acme told us about pricing?" because their tags are organized by study and theme, not by company. The data is present and unreachable.

## In UpSight

UpSight organizes around people and relationships rather than studies and tags, which is what makes the per-customer question answerable without a maintained taxonomy.

## FAQ

**What's the difference between a repository and a customer intelligence platform?**

A repository stores research artifacts organized by study. A customer intelligence platform organizes what was learned around the people who said it, and keeps accumulating between studies.

**Do we need a research repository?**

If multiple people run research and studies recur, you need the function. Whether it needs to be a dedicated tool depends on whether anything else already holds the same evidence.

**Why do research repositories go stale?**

They depend on manual tagging discipline that competes with the actual research work, and the discipline loses.
