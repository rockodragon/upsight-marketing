# What is an evidence log?

**An evidence log is a running, dated record of what customers actually said — verbatim and attributed — that serves as the source of truth behind product, pricing, and go-to-market decisions.**

The format is deliberately boring: date, person, source (call, survey, interview), the quote itself, and a tag or two. What makes it useful is the discipline of adding to it in the moment rather than reconstructing it later from memory, and never editing an entry once written.

## Why it matters

Memory reorders evidence to fit the conclusion you have since reached. A log written at the time is the only defense. It also makes onboarding, investor conversations, and "why did we decide that?" questions cheap to answer six months later.

## Example

A founder reviewing a quarter of entries notices that the phrase "I don't trust the number" appears in five separate logs from five different companies. No individual conversation felt like a trust problem. The log made the pattern visible.

## FAQ

**What goes in an evidence log entry?**

Date, who said it, where (call, survey, interview), the verbatim quote, and the theme it relates to. Keep interpretation in a separate field from the quote.

**Should I log everything?**

Log anything surprising, anything that contradicts a current assumption, and anything a customer repeats. Logging all of it guarantees reading none of it.

**Is an evidence log the same as meeting notes?**

No. Meeting notes summarize a conversation. An evidence log preserves the specific claims across conversations so patterns become visible.
