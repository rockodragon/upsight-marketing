# What is an opportunity solution tree?

**An opportunity solution tree is a visual map that connects a single desired outcome to the customer opportunities that could move it, the candidate solutions for each opportunity, and the experiments that would test those solutions.**

The structure has four levels: outcome at the top, opportunities beneath it, solutions beneath those, and experiments at the bottom. Opportunities are unmet needs, pains, and desires expressed by customers — phrased in their words, not as features. The tree is developed by Teresa Torres as the central artifact of continuous discovery.

## Why it matters

It forces two useful separations. First, between the problem space and the solution space, which teams collapse constantly. Second, between competing solutions to the *same* opportunity, which is where genuine option value lives — most teams generate one idea per problem and then defend it.

## Example

Outcome: increase weekly active teams. Opportunity: "I can't tell if anyone read my report." Solutions: read receipts, a digest email, an in-app activity feed. Experiments: a fake-door test for each. The opportunity came from interview quotes; the solutions did not.

Opportunities in UpSight stay linked to the evidence that produced them, so a branch of the tree can be opened to see the quotes and the people behind it.

## FAQ

**What goes at the top of an opportunity solution tree?**

One measurable outcome. Trees with multiple outcomes at the top become unmanageable and hide trade-offs.

**What's the difference between an opportunity and a solution?**

An opportunity is a customer need stated in the customer's terms. A solution is something you could build. "I never know if it worked" is an opportunity; "add a dashboard" is a solution.

**How often should the tree be updated?**

Continuously — it is a working artifact, not a deliverable. New interview evidence should change it weekly.
