# What is customer evidence?

**Customer evidence is a specific, attributable thing a customer said or did — a verbatim quote, a survey answer, an observed behavior — kept in its original form so that any conclusion drawn from it can be traced back and checked.**

Evidence has three properties that an opinion does not: it has a source, it has a date, and it can be re-read by someone who disagrees with your interpretation. A claim that cannot be opened to reveal its underlying quote is not evidence; it is a summary that has lost its receipt.

## Why it matters

Roadmap arguments are usually not disagreements about facts. They are disagreements about whose anecdote is representative. Evidence with attribution ends that argument quickly: the question becomes how many people said this and who they were, which is answerable.

## Example

"Customers want better reporting" is an opinion. "Seven of 19 interviewees, all in the 10-50 employee segment, said they export to a spreadsheet before every board meeting — here are the seven quotes" is evidence. The second one also tells you what to build.

## FAQ

**What counts as customer evidence?**

Anything attributable and dated: a quote from a recorded call, a written survey response, a support ticket, an observed action in product analytics.

**How much evidence is enough to act?**

Enough that the pattern repeats across people who are not in the same company or role. One customer saying something three times is still one data point.

**Why does attribution matter so much?**

Without it you cannot tell whether a theme is a market pattern or a single loud customer, and those require opposite responses.
