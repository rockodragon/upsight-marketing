# What is jobs to be done (JTBD)?

**Jobs to be done (JTBD) is a framework that defines a product by the progress a customer is trying to make in a particular circumstance — the "job" they hire the product to do — rather than by the customer's demographics or the product's features.**

The framing, associated with Clayton Christensen and Bob Moesta among others, reframes competition. If the job is "help me feel like I made a responsible decision," your competitor may be a spreadsheet, an analyst report, or doing nothing — none of which appear in a feature comparison.

## Why it matters

Feature-based thinking produces roadmaps that match competitors. Job-based thinking reveals why customers actually switched, which is usually a struggling moment rather than a missing feature. It also explains non-consumption: the largest competitor for most products is the status quo.

## Example

The canonical example: people "hire" a morning milkshake to make a boring commute tolerable with one hand on the wheel. Its competitors are bananas and bagels, not other milkshakes. Improving the milkshake as a milkshake misses entirely.

## FAQ

**What is jobs to be done in simple terms?**

People don't buy products, they hire them to make progress in a specific situation. Understand the situation and the progress, not the buyer's job title.

**How do you run a JTBD interview?**

Reconstruct the timeline of a real purchase: first thought, passive looking, active looking, the deciding event, and what they were anxious about.

**Is JTBD the same as a user story?**

No. A user story describes a desired feature interaction. A job describes the progress the person wants regardless of what product delivers it.
