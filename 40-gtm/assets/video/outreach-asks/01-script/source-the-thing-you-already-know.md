# UpSight VSL — "The Thing You Already Know"

**Length:** 3:14 · **Use:** homepage hero, paid traffic, first sales touch
**Job:** the prospect recognizes their own situation in the first 20 seconds, and by the end
wants the loop running on their own customers.

**The one idea:** *You already had the answer. It was in a conversation nobody could search.*

**Tone:** confident, quiet, a little haunting at the open. No hype, no stock-photo optimism, no
"AI-powered." The product is the proof, not the promise.

Canonical timed copy lives in `hero.script.json`. This file keeps the creative argument,
evidence base, production notes, and variants.

## Why this works

- **Act I** is not a pain point, it's a memory. Every operator has this scar. Do not put the logo here.
- **Act II** villain is *non-accumulation*, not laziness or bad tools. That reframe is what separates us from note-takers and survey tools.
- **Act III** logo lands as relief, not interruption. "Play it back" beats every competitor's "insights."
- **Beat 1** commercially: every competitor's ceiling is your team's calendar. Ours isn't. The disclosure line is peer-reviewed (Lucas et al., 2014).
- **Beat 1b** is the proof of compounding — the system *declining to ask* because it already knows.
- **Beat 5** is the money shot: the row leaving Waiting-on, and the counter ticking up. Every competitor demo ends at an insight. Ours ends with a meeting booked and the system knowing more than it did ninety seconds ago.
- **CTA** is "Upload a conversation" — lowest-friction proof in the category.

## Evidence base

Do not use internal product metrics in marketing. Published, peer-reviewed, independent:

| Claim in the film | Source | What it actually found |
|---|---|---|
| People disclose more to an AI than to a person (Beat 1) | Lucas, Gratch, King & Morency (2014), *Computers in Human Behavior* 37:94–100 | Lower fear of self-disclosure, lower impression management, observers rated more willing to disclose. |
| People say more out loud than they'll type (Beat 1) | Höhne, Gavras & Claassen (2024), *Social Science Computer Review* | Voice answers contained more words and covered more topics. |
| Spoken answers are substantially longer | Multiple survey-methodology studies | Voice responses ~40–70% longer than typed. |
| A conversation beats a form (Beat 1) | Kim, Lee & Gweon (2019), *CHI '19*; Xiao et al. | Conversational format reduced satisficing, higher engagement, better open-ended data. |
| Let them choose how to answer | Same literature | Willingness to use voice varies widely — offering the choice outperforms mandating one mode. |

**What NOT to claim:** that more people respond to voice than to a form. Depth and candor, not volume.

## Production notes

- Cast real customers, not actors. v1 uses an illustrative named cast until capture lands.
- Every number on screen must be real in production. v1 is labeled illustrative in notes, not on screen.
- No feature names on screen (not "themes," not "lenses," not "gen-UI").
- Pace: slow open, fast middle, slow close.
- Silence is a tool.
