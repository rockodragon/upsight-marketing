The problem

The report is due. The evidence is scattered.

Every program comes with a report

CDBG, SBA, EDA and state grants all ask the same things: how many businesses you helped, what changed for them, and how you know.

The data you can buy is regional and old

Purchased datasets describe counties and industries, six to twenty-four months late. They cannot tell you what is happening on your corridor this quarter.

Studies end and the baseline restarts

A consultant study is a snapshot. When it ends, the conversations go into a PDF and the next study starts from zero.

Year two

Why it gets better every quarter

The report shows what changed

The same businesses, asked against their own earlier answers. The filing shows the change per business, with both quotes side by side.

Early warning

Lease renewals bunching up, suppliers tightening terms, owners retiring with no successor. You hear it before it shows up in sales-tax data.

A record that survives staff turnover

When the person who ran the program moves on, the history of every business stays.

Less of the owner's time each cycle

Year one asks twenty questions. Year two asks four, because we already know the other sixteen answers.
