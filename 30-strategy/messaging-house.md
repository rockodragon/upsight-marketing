## Core promise

**UpSight prepares your weekly leadership meeting from the conversations your team is already having.**

Short variant:

**Your weekly leadership meeting, already prepared.**

This is the expansion promise people should be able to repeat once they understand and trust the
customer-feedback layer.

## Strategic framing

- The **product** is continuous operating intelligence.
- The **weekly meeting** is where customers feel that value immediately.
- The meeting is **the proof**, not the entire product.

That distinction matters:

- It keeps the public story concrete.
- It keeps the product roadmap broader than "meeting notes."
- It gives every other feature a job inside one coherent narrative.

## Value proposition

UpSight automatically prepares this week's numbers, priorities, issues, and action items before leadership meets, with evidence behind every recommendation.

The outcome is simple:

- teams spend less time collecting updates
- teams spend more time making decisions

## Three pillars

### 1. Automatically prepared

The meeting is ready before the team walks in.

Signals:

- scorecard and numbers
- priorities and carry-over
- issues to discuss
- action items with owners

### 2. Grounded in evidence

Every important claim should link back to what was actually said.

Signals:

- quotes and attribution
- source links and replay
- receipts behind recommendations
- trust in the assembled agenda

### 3. Gets smarter every week

The system improves as the team keeps operating.

Signals:

- compounding meeting memory
- patterns and trend detection
- conversation analysis over time
- less setup as more activity flows through

## The memorable proof line

Use these often:

- **Every issue has evidence.**
- **Every action has an owner.**
- **Every meeting starts ready.**

These are sharper than generic "AI customer intelligence" language.

## Category

For dedicated operating-cadence messaging, lead with:

**Leadership meeting preparation**

or

**Operating meeting preparation**

Do not lead an operating-cadence page with:

- AI interviews
- survey software
- conversation intelligence
- CRM replacement
- EOS-only language

Those are supporting contexts, not the first frame.

## Audience routing

### Expansion-page audience

Lead with the operator / leadership-team use case:

- CEO / founder
- COO / chief of staff
- fractional COO / operator

### Earlier customer-feedback audiences

Keep these as secondary entry points:

- product and research teams
- founder-led sellers
- consultants and fractional strategists
- agent-native / MCP users

Rule:

If the story starts with customer calls, surveys, or feedback analysis, route it through the current
homepage messaging house instead of this expansion story.

## Feature mapping

Every feature should map to a pillar.

| Feature | Pillar |
|---|---|
| Scorecard / numbers | Automatically prepared |
| Priorities / rocks / carry-over | Automatically prepared |
| Issues / discussion queue | Automatically prepared |
| Action items / task handoff | Automatically prepared |
| Meeting capture | Grounded in evidence |
| AI interviews | Grounded in evidence |
| Surveys | Grounded in evidence |
| Voice AI / conversation replay | Grounded in evidence |
| Pattern detection / trends | Gets smarter every week |
| Chat / synthesis / memory | Gets smarter every week |

If a feature cannot clearly strengthen a pillar, do not lead with it in marketing.

## Dedicated page rules

Above the fold, answer only these questions:

1. What is it?
2. Why trust it?
3. How long does setup take?
4. Can I see the meeting it prepares?

Recommended CTA direction:

- **See this week's meeting**
- **See a prepared meeting**

Less concrete:

- "See your first meeting"
- generic "learn more"

## EOS rule

Do not lead with EOS in the hero.

Use broader language first:

- weekly leadership meeting
- operating meeting
- Monday leadership meeting

Then bring EOS lower on the page or in supporting proof:

**Works with EOS, Ninety, Bloom, and your existing operating cadence.**
