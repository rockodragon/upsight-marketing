# Pricing and business model — The Decision Files

**Date:** 2026-08-18 · **Status:** money-layer model, built on top of `competitive-landscape.md` (this week's landscape memo), `decision-files-gameplan.md`, and `business-model-decision-files.md`. Does not redo the landscape work — takes its whitespace claim as given and prices it.

**The two-sentence verdict, up front:** This is marketing wearing business clothes today, and the model below only clears standalone-business thresholds in the BASE/HIGH cases — both of which require the contributor board and the quarterly-report "forced-participation" mechanic to actually work, neither of which exists yet as of this writing. Fund and run it as an UpSight demand-gen line now, with the explicit monthly triggers in §5 as the switch that converts it to a standalone P&L the moment real numbers land, not a hope.

---

## 0. What already exists, so this doc doesn't re-litigate it

`business-model-decision-files.md` already set the **bespoke engagement** pricing (Pilot $15–25K, Standard $35–50K, Retainer $60–100K/yr) and the **A/B tool-adoption test** (report-only $25K vs. report-plus-workspace $35K). That stands. This doc adds the layer Rick asked for: what a **subscription** — reader, team, or vendor — is worth, priced against real comparables, plus a LOW/BASE/HIGH model that puts a number on all three revenue lines together, a business-vs-marketing verdict with thresholds, and a moat ranking.

`40-gtm/channels/content-seo/decision-files-site-build-spec.md` already decided that **individual files stay free forever** — only the quarterly synthesis report and the pre-quarter archive are gated, and today that gate is an **email address, not a payment**. The pricing tiers below are a paid layer proposed on top of that free/email-gated base, not a replacement for it — same shape as The Information's split between free-to-register, a $399/yr standard tier, and a $10K/yr institutional tier (see §1).

## 3. Pricing-tier proposal — reader / team / vendor

This is a **new paid layer**, additive to the existing free-file / email-gated-report design in the site build spec, and separate from the bespoke engagement tiers already priced in `business-model-decision-files.md` (those stay as-is — see the note in §0).

| Tier | Price | Who | What they get | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Free** (unchanged) | $0 | Anyone | Every individual Decision File, forever, no wall | Existing site-build-spec decision — not revisited here |
| **Reader+** | **$299/yr** ($29/mo) | Individual practitioner (architect, engineering lead, CISO) | Full quarterly-report archive, no email-gate friction, early access (files ~1 week before public release) | Between Lenny's/Pragmatic Engineer ($150–180/yr, mass-audience price) and The Information's standard tier ($399/yr, narrow-B2B price) — Decision Files' audience is narrower than either, so it should price toward the narrow end, not the mass end |
| **Team** | **$2,500/yr** (up to 5 seats) / **$4,500/yr** (up to 10 seats) | PR/marketing agencies (per `business-model-decision-files.md`'s own note that a single agency "carries 20–40 vendor clients"), boutique research/consulting shops, an internal product-marketing or competitive-intel team | Everything in Reader+, shared login, one shared Slack/email digest | ~$500/seat — a small fraction of Gartner/IDC/Tegus per-seat pricing ($15–50K), explicitly marketed as "the whole team for less than one Gartner seat" |
| **Vendor (observer)** | **$15,000/yr** | A security vendor's product marketing/CRO team, self-serve — not a bespoke engagement | Full archive access under company license, quarterly category-rollup reports first, vendor-mention alerts across the corpus. **No influence over selection, questions, or findings** — same firewall as the bespoke tier | Sits below GLG's subscription floor ($50–150K) and below the bespoke Pilot engagement ($15–25K) — a lighter-commitment, self-serve entry point that can convert upward into a bespoke engagement, mirroring how a GLG or Tegus subscription is the entry point before a paid custom project |

**Why this shape, not something more aggressive:** Gartner/Forrester/IDC are opaque and expensive; Tegus/GLG are opaque, expensive, and investor-only; Lenny's/Stratechery/Pragmatic Engineer are transparent and cheap but need 1M+-reader mass audiences Decision Files' security-only niche doesn't have yet. The proposal above is deliberately positioned as **transparent pricing at roughly 1–5% of the analyst-firm comparables** — cheap enough to be a no-brainer for a practitioner or small team, priced high enough on the vendor tier that it reads as a serious B2B product, not a newsletter tip jar. Publishing the price itself (unlike every analyst-firm comparable in §1) is a positioning move, not just a pricing one.

## 4. Value + take-rate model — LOW / BASE / HIGH, security vertical only

**Important framing caveat:** this is a **steady-state annualized model**, roughly 18–24 months into execution with a working contributor board (per `decision-files-advisory-board.md`'s "10/month becomes 30 without hiring" capacity claim) — **not** a year-one number. The gameplan doc's own 90-day target is three published files and one paid conversation; nothing below should be read as a year-one forecast.

**Audience sizing (a):** ~1.34M US cybersecurity workers ([stationx.net](https://app.stationx.net/articles/cybersecurity-job-market-statistics)); of those, an estimated 10–15% hold roles with real vendor-selection influence (architects, engineering leads, directors, CISOs) → **~150,000–200,000** addressable North American readers. There are only ~35,000 CISOs globally ([natlawreview.com](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/35000-chief-information-security-officers-employed-globally-2026), [cybersecurityventures.com](https://cybersecurityventures.com/why-the-ciso-role-is-becoming-more-demanding-in-2026/)) — too thin a population to price a subscription against alone, which is why the broader "vendor-evaluation-influence" population is the right denominator, not CISO title count.

**Vendor universe sizing (b):** IT-Harvest tracks **~4,100–4,500 cybersecurity vendors** ([it-harvest.com/about](https://it-harvest.com/about), [security-yearbook.com](https://www.security-yearbook.com/)). `business-model-decision-files.md` explicitly targets the **$10–100M revenue segment** (real budget, real win/loss pain, not yet a category giant with an internal competitive-intel team). No public source breaks vendor count down by revenue band; a reasoned estimate — most of the 4,100+ are pre-revenue or early-stage, a minority are >$100M — puts that segment at roughly **700–800 companies**. Treat this as an estimate, not a sourced figure.

**Conversion assumptions:** take rates below are deliberately conservative relative to the comparables in §1 — Decision Files is years away from Lenny's/Stratechery audience scale, and `business-model-decision-files.md` itself flags the engagement-to-license conversion as untested.

| Revenue line | LOW | BASE | HIGH |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Reader + Team subscriptions** — $299/yr × readers, ~$500/seat × teams | **$60K** (150 readers @ 0.1% take of 150K + 5 teams) | **$195K** (450 readers @ 0.3% take + 20 teams) | **$780K** (2,000 readers @ 0.8% take of 250K + 60 teams) |
| **(b) Vendor-paid candor** — bespoke Pilot/Standard/Retainer engagements (per `business-model-decision-files.md`) + Vendor-observer subscriptions ($15K) + quarterly category-report sponsorship ($15–20K/sponsor, 2–4 sponsors/report, firewall intact) | **$70K** (2 Pilot engagements + 2 vendor subs; no report line yet) | **$435K** (6 blended bespoke engagements ≈ $225K + 8 vendor subs ≈ $120K + 2 sponsored reports ≈ $90K) | **$1.3M** (15 blended bespoke engagements ≈ $640K + 25 vendor subs ≈ $375K + 4 sponsored reports ≈ $290K) |
| **(c) Software pull-through — CAC displacement only** (excludes multi-year license LTV, which is additive and larger; this line strictly answers "what CAC did UpSight avoid") | **$2K** (2 bespoke engagements × 10% convert × $10K CAC avoided) | **$30K** (8 × 25% × $15K) | **$140K** (18 × 40% × $20K) |
| **Total, single vertical (security)** | **≈$130K** | **≈$660K** | **≈$2.2M** |

**CAC-avoided figure (c) sourced from:** enterprise-tier B2B security software CAC benchmarks — $10,221 reported enterprise-segment average, $5,000–$250,000+ range for >$100K ACV enterprise SaaS generally ([tomba.io](https://tomba.io/blog/customer-acquisition-cost-by-industry), [ltvcacbook.com](https://ltvcacbook.com/blog/cac-benchmarks-2026)). $10–20K blended is conservative within that range.

**What this table actually says:** even LOW (~$130K/yr) is a real number a solo operator with a part-time board could plausibly hit — it's roughly the "very good one-person consulting practice" outcome the gameplan doc already names as a legitimate result. BASE (~$660K/yr) and HIGH (~$2.2M/yr) both require the vendor-subscription and category-report products in §3 to exist and sell, which today they do not — they're proposed, not built. **(c) is small relative to (a)+(b) in every scenario (≤7% of total even at HIGH)** — meaning the "software pull-through" value Rick asked to size is real but modest; Decision Files' dominant value, even under generous assumptions, is as its own revenue lines, not primarily as an UpSight lead-gen mechanic. That's the strongest evidence for treating it as more than marketing — and simultaneously the reason not to over-fund it as marketing at the expense of building it as a business.

## 5. Is this a business or just marketing?

### Steelman: it's a real business

- BASE case (~$660K/yr, single vertical) is a fundable, headcount-justifying number; HIGH (~$2.2M/yr) is a real Series-A-adjacent business line.
- The bespoke-engagement pricing is not invented — it sits squarely inside the Clozd ($50K+/yr) and Primary Intelligence ($40–90K/yr, avg. $65K) comparables. This is underpricing a proven market, not guessing at one.
- SemiAnalysis (§1) is a live proof that "narrow reader subscription + much larger paid institutional research" scales past $100M/yr in a technical vertical — the exact shape being proposed here, one vertical at a time.
- If the quarterly category-report "forced-participation" mechanic (the doc's own "still undecided #3") can be engineered, the whole model becomes closer to the Gartner precedent it's positioned against — a genuine franchise, not a services shop with a newsletter.

### Steelman: it's a marketing channel for UpSight

- `business-model-decision-files.md` already names the trap explicitly: bespoke engagements are "consulting... lumpy, it's Rick's hours, and it competes directly with the time needed to build UpSight."
- The entire moat premise (§6) — the named contributor board — is still hypothetical as of the 2026-08-18 update in `decision-files-gameplan.md`: the first masthead ask (Chenxi Wang) was explicitly decided against, and "3 yeses" have not yet landed. Nothing in §4's BASE/HIGH case works without that board existing.
- The gameplan doc's own kill criteria (two failed publish cycles; engagements not converting to UpSight licenses by engagement #3) show the org already treats this as provisional, not committed.
- Rick's own board-less capacity is capped at ~2.5 hrs/month per `decision-files-advisory-board.md` — a hard ceiling on (b) until the board is real, meaning LOW is the only case reachable without new hires or a functioning board.
- (c) is ≤7% of total value in every scenario — the "it's demand-gen for UpSight" framing undersells what this could be worth on its own, which argues for deciding rather than defaulting into either label.

### The verdict, with numeric thresholds

| Signal | **Proves standalone business** — fund/build as its own line, consider headcount | **Content-channel zone** — run and budget as an UpSight marketing line item |
|---|---|---|
| Combined monthly revenue run-rate | **≥$40,000/month**, sustained 3 consecutive months | **<$10,000/month** after 12 months of the paid tier (§3) being live |
| Paying Reader+/Team subscribers | ≥300 in a rolling 12-month window | — |
| Vendor deals (bespoke + subscription) | ≥6 signed in a rolling 12-month window, or ≥2/quarter for 2 consecutive quarters | <2/quarter for 2+ consecutive quarters |
| Zone between the two | **$10,000–$40,000/month**: not yet decided — re-evaluate quarterly against the trend, don't force a label early |

These thresholds map directly onto §4: LOW (~$130K/yr ≈ $11K/month) sits just inside the content-channel floor; BASE (~$660K/yr ≈ $55K/month) clears the standalone-business bar comfortably. **The honest read today: nothing in the current state (pre-board, pre-paid-tier, three files targeted at 90 days) has generated the data to place this above the "not yet decided" zone.** Fund it as UpSight marketing now, instrument the thresholds from day one of the paid tier launching, and let the numbers — not the org chart — decide when it graduates.

## 6. Moat ranking — which candidates are real at small scale

Ranked by how much they actually protect the business **today**, at 3–10 published files, not at the eventual scale where volume-dependent moats would kick in.

| Rank | Candidate | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Named contributor board** | **Real, today.** This is the doc's own stated thesis ("the board is the moat... access is easy, candor is scarce") and it's the correct one: a funded competitor can buy interview volume but cannot buy Gary Hayslip's or Trey Ford's credibility with other CISOs — that has to be earned, takes years, and directly gates the raw material (candid interviews) everything else depends on. Weakest link: it doesn't exist yet — "3 yeses" is still pending as of the 2026-08-18 gameplan update. |
| **2. UpSight tooling cost advantage** (~$0.03 compute + one 15-min interview vs. analyst-days) | **Real, but its payoff is deferred.** The cost-structure gap versus Clozd/Primary Intelligence (people-bound cost) is genuine and compounds as the software improves and the interview corpus grows. But at 3–10 files/year the advantage is theoretical — it starts actually separating Decision Files from a well-funded copier around 50–100+ files/year, which is a scale question, not a today question. |
| **3. Receipts/verifiability standard** | **Real differentiation, weak defense.** `competitive-landscape.md` confirms nobody else combines public + decision-level + receipts — but it's a *process discipline*, not a scarce asset. Anything that can be copied by a motivated, funded actor (and the landscape memo names two: a win-loss vendor publishing instead of privately delivering, or Tegus/AlphaSense pointing existing interview infrastructure at a public audience) isn't a moat in the economic sense, even though it's a genuinely good editorial standard. |
| **4. File corpus as citable public record** | **Real eventually, not yet.** Citation/backlink/LLM-training-corpus moats need mass — Stratechery's decade-plus archive is why it's citable by default; three to ten files isn't yet enough density to be the thing search engines or models reach for. This becomes real around the same 50–100+ file mark as #2. |
| **5. Quarterly rollup reports as credential** | **The highest ceiling, the least proven.** This is explicitly the doc's own "still undecided" forced-participation lever — the one design element that could turn this into an NSS-style franchise rather than a good consulting practice. At today's file count it structurally can't yet create "conspicuous by absence" pressure on any vendor, because there isn't yet a category with enough coverage density to be watched. Worth building deliberately (it's the biggest single swing factor in the HIGH case in §4), but rank it last for what it protects *right now*, which is nothing. |

**One-line summary:** the board is the only candidate on this list that's already doing real work at N=3 files; everything else either compounds later (tooling cost, corpus mass) or is unbuilt and speculative but potentially the biggest lever (the quarterly report). Don't let "receipts" — the easiest one to talk about — stand in for the two that actually matter.
