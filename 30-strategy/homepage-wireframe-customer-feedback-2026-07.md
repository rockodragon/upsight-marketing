# UpSight Homepage Wireframe - Customer Intelligence V4

> Production-copy companion to the coded prototype in
> `40-gtm/assets/homepage/customer-intelligence-2026-07/`. V4 keeps the category-first V3 position
> and adds a Fletch-style champion plus the disciplined proof-and-conversion sequence demonstrated
> by the Standard Issue template. It does not copy the template's visual identity or unsupported
> proof sections.

## Positioning decision

Enter the established category plainly:

**Customer intelligence platform**

Then state the human benefit and UpSight distinction:

**Know your customers. Not just their feedback.**

UpSight brings calls, surveys, interviews, and notes together so product, leadership, and marketing
can see what customers need, why it matters, and who said it.

This follows the strongest competitive pattern: simple category language, a short point of view,
one dominant product visual, and tangible jobs. The homepage should not ask visitors to decode
“compounding customer knowledge” before telling them what the product is.

## Positioning map

| Element | Homepage decision |
|---|---|
| Champion | Founder or head of product at a lean B2B company already talking to customers |
| Context | Customer knowledge is scattered across calls, forms, notes, a CRM, and documents |
| Market category | Customer intelligence platform |
| Current solution | Notetaker + survey + research repository + CRM/sheet + story workflow |
| Current limitation | Each tool remembers a fragment; no one remembers the person over time |
| UpSight distinction | Living customer profiles that connect interactions and improve what happens next |
| Immediate benefit | See what several conversations add up to, with inspectable evidence |
| Downstream value | Ask better questions, make defensible decisions, and share authentic stories |

## Conversion

### Primary path

**Analyze 3 conversations**

Support:

**Start with conversations you already have. No blank survey required.**

One input demonstrates summarization. Three begin to demonstrate cross-conversation customer
intelligence. Keep the number, but use the verb **analyze** rather than making the file-transfer
mechanic the value proposition.

### Assisted path

**Analyze them with Rick**

This remains a concrete working session rather than a generic demo.

## Homepage sequence

### 1. Champion, category, outcome, and proof

Label:

**Customer intelligence for lean B2B teams**

Headline:

**Know your customers. Not just their feedback.**

Body:

**UpSight brings calls, surveys, interviews, and notes together so product, leadership, and
marketing can see what customers need, why it matters, and who said it.**

Reasons to believe:

- **Build a living profile for every customer.**
- **Find patterns across conversations with evidence attached.**
- **Turn the same insight into decisions and customer stories.**

Primary CTA: **Analyze 3 conversations**

Assisted CTA: **Analyze them with Rick**

Risk reversal:

**Use conversations you already have. No blank survey, no generic demo.**

Place the existing `StreamingMarquee` Remotion asset beside this copy. It should visibly show several
real conversations becoming themes, takeaways, and source evidence. This earns the claim before the
visitor reaches a secondary section.

### 2. Old way versus UpSight way

Headline:

**There is a better way to understand customers.**

Old way:

**Five disconnected tools. Every interaction starts from a fragment.**

- a notetaker remembers one call
- a survey tool remembers one response
- a repository remembers what someone tagged
- a CRM or spreadsheet remembers static fields
- a testimonial workflow asks for the story again

UpSight way:

**One customer-intelligence layer. Every interaction strengthens what you know.**

- every signal stays connected to the person
- new conversations enrich the existing profile
- patterns stay linked to source evidence
- prior context improves the next question
- the same truth supports decisions and stories

Claim boundary:

**UpSight collapses the handoffs across five tools. It does not claim complete feature parity with
five mature software categories.**

### 3. Three product jobs

Use familiar market language instead of inventing a branded framework.

#### Unify

**Every signal, attached to the person behind it.**

Show the real person-profile artifact. Explain one history across interactions, source evidence, and
context that gets richer over time.

#### Understand

**See what one customer needs and what many are telling you.**

Show the real themes artifact. Explain cross-conversation patterns, weak signals, and inspectable
evidence.

#### Act

**Put customer truth to work.**

Action includes:

- **Ask:** smarter follow-ups based on prior context
- **Decide:** what to build, change, price, or prioritize
- **Share:** customer stories for marketing, impact reports, sponsors, and donors

This keeps “act” market-facing. Publishing a story and communicating impact are actions, not only
internal task management.

### 4. Differentiating mechanism

Headline:

**Customer intelligence should not reset.**

Show the progression from first conversation to next response, follow-up, and long-term context.
This is where the prior V2 “compounding” idea belongs: as a product mechanism after the visitor
already understands the category.

### 5. Concrete path to first value

Headline:

**Get from scattered conversations to a shared answer in 3 steps.**

1. **Bring conversations.** Start with calls, surveys, interviews, or notes you already have.
2. **Let UpSight connect them.** Profiles, themes, and source evidence form across interactions.
3. **Inspect and put them to work.** Validate the finding, make a decision, or share the story.

Repeat **Analyze 3 conversations** and the evidence-first risk reversal. Do not promise a specific
migration time until the real activation path supports it.

### 6. Founder conviction

Headline:

**Customers should not have to repeat themselves.**

Explain why UpSight exists in the founder's voice: customer understanding should persist across
tools and teams, and evidence should remain attached to the person behind it.

Pull quote:

**Remember the customer. Not just the conversation.**

Sign it **Rick Moy, Founder of UpSight**. This is conviction, not a substitute for customer proof.

### 7. Final conversion

Headline:

**Bring the conversations you already have.**

Body:

**UpSight will show you the customers, patterns, and proof already inside them.**

Primary: **Analyze 3 conversations**

Secondary: **Analyze them with Rick**

## Visual direction

The V4 visual system should feel like a confident product company with a deliberate conversion
path, not a long-form AI strategy page:

- light, high-whitespace split hero with copy and live multi-customer proof
- direct old-way/new-way comparison with thin rules and restrained contrast
- three large product chapters instead of repeated cards
- one amber interruption for the persistent-memory mechanism
- numbered three-step setup and a founder manifesto
- real product artifacts at meaningful scale
- short declarative copy blocks
- no gradients, glows, glass cards, stock images, or decorative AI diagrams
- no fake logos, metrics, or testimonials
- no more than one major visual idea per viewport

## Competitive lesson

- Dovetail succeeds with category confidence, a sharp point of view, product proof, and measurable
  business value.
- Enterpret succeeds with familiar “unify, understand, act” language and enterprise context.
- Listen Labs succeeds through extreme simplicity: understand what people want, why, and fast.
- Fletch succeeds by making the champion, category, problem, and value unambiguous before writing
  production homepage copy.
- Standard Issue succeeds through persuasion order: problem and outcome, reasons to believe,
  comparison, product, setup, risk reversal, founder conviction, and final conversion.
- UpSight should borrow that clarity, not their exact claims or visual identity.

The goal is not to “skate ahead” of the category. It is to look credible within it, then make the
person-level continuity and real customer voices impossible to miss.

## Proof debt before launch

1. Verify that three inputs visibly generate a cross-conversation pattern.
2. Verify that the customer profile enriches across interactions.
3. Show one prior-context follow-up question in the product.
4. Add one named customer outcome or measured activation result.
5. Add a verified privacy statement for uploaded customer data.
