# Homepage Messaging Reconciliation — Customer Intelligence First

> Purpose: reconcile the current homepage/story conflict and recommend the right message hierarchy
> for the next `getupsight.com` homepage.

> **Superseded for homepage implementation:** This memo correctly moved EOS out of the acquisition
> story and contains the initial ICP and unit-economics pressure test. Its hero and page sequence were
> replaced after an outside competitive audit showed that feedback analysis, citations, and story
> outputs were not sufficiently differentiated. A later V3 correction also moved the compounding-
> profile mechanism below the category promise. Use
> `homepage-outside-critical-analysis-2026-07-19.md`,
> `homepage-wireframe-customer-feedback-2026-07.md`, and
> `messaging-house-customer-intelligence.md` for the current V3 position: **customer intelligence
> platform** / **Know your customers. Not just their feedback.**

## Bottom line

**Recommendation: lead the homepage with customer understanding, not the weekly leadership meeting.**

The new homepage should make a smaller, faster-to-believe promise:

**Know your customers better. Share their stories with confidence.**

Then prove it with three concrete product truths:

1. UpSight captures customer signal across calls, surveys, interviews, and event conversations.
2. Every insight is grounded in evidence you can click into.
3. The output is not just analysis. It is team alignment and reusable customer proof: testimonials,
   impact stories, sponsor proof, investor/donor reporting, and better decisions.

**EOS / weekly operating cadence stays in the product story, but moves below the fold or to a
dedicated operating-system page.**

## What this reconciles

As of **July 19, 2026**, the vault has two competing homepage-level stories:

- `30-strategy/messaging-house.md` says the public wedge is:
  **"Your weekly leadership meeting, already prepared."**
- `30-strategy/messaging-house-customer-intelligence.md` argues for a simpler entry point:
  **capture -> understanding**, before the operating-cadence promise.

The live homepage metadata on **July 19, 2026** is already closer to the second story than the
first:

- Title: **"UpSight — Customer Intelligence with Receipts"**
- Description: **"Turn customer conversations into verifiable evidence your whole team can search and act on..."**

So the practical recommendation is not "invent a third story."

It is:

**Promote the customer-intelligence wedge to the homepage lead, and demote the weekly-meeting /
EOS story to a secondary layer.**

## Why this is the right move

### 1. It matches the product users can understand fastest

"Know your customers better" is a lower-adoption ask than "change how your leadership team runs
every week."

The weekly-meeting promise is strong once someone already believes the data layer. It is too big as
a first impression for cold traffic.

### 2. It better fits the survey + conversation pipeline work

The most differentiated thread in the current product is not generic meeting prep.

It is:

- conversational capture
- adaptive surveys
- cross-conversation memory
- evidence-backed synthesis
- authentic customer stories in text, audio, and video

That is a sharper public story than "AI for EOS meetings."

### 3. It creates a cleaner message ladder

The message sequence should be:

1. **Understand customers**
2. **Trust the evidence**
3. **Share what you learned**
4. **Act on it**
5. **Operationalize it weekly**

Right now the docs often jump straight to step 5.

### 4. It protects against category confusion

Your rough problem-solution notes currently mix at least four products:

- customer intelligence / research / survey system
- team memory / personal CRM
- sales deal guidance / next best action
- leadership operating system / EOS prep

Those may all be valid product surfaces. They should **not** all be on the homepage.

If the homepage tries to sell all four, visitors will understand none of them.

## Recommended homepage promise

### Recommended hero

**Know your customers better. Share their stories with confidence.**

Subhead:

**UpSight turns calls, surveys, interviews, and event conversations into evidence your team can
trust, decisions you can defend, and authentic customer stories you can reuse in testimonials,
impact reports, and internal alignment.**

### Strong alternate hero

**Your customers already told you what matters. UpSight helps your whole team hear it.**

### Strong alternate hero with more "receipts"

**Customer intelligence with receipts.**

Subhead:

**See what customers need, why they say it, and who said it, across every conversation and
survey. Then turn that evidence into better decisions and better stories.**

## Recommended message hierarchy

### 1. Primary message: know customers better

This is the homepage job.

Use language like:

- understand what customers need
- see the themes that matter
- know which voices are missing
- stop guessing what customers want

### 2. Proof message: every insight has evidence

This is the main trust builder.

Use language like:

- every theme has a quote
- every claim links back to a real person
- evidence you can click into
- insights with receipts

### 3. Outcome message: share stories internally and externally

This is where the homepage gets more commercially specific.

Use language like:

- share customer truth with product, marketing, leadership, and programs
- turn responses into testimonials, case studies, and impact stories
- give donors, sponsors, and stakeholders real customer voice, not generic summaries

Important nuance:

**"Donors" should appear as an example of who benefits from better stories, not as the primary
homepage audience.**

### 4. Expansion message: turn insight into action

This should exist, but lower on the page.

Use language like:

- decide what to build, change, or say next
- prioritize the most important follow-up
- turn insight into action

### 5. Later-stage message: weekly operating cadence / EOS

This moves below the fold.

Use language like:

- works with your weekly operating rhythm
- helps teams turn customer understanding into recurring decisions
- compatible with EOS, Ninety, Bloom, and your own cadence

## What to keep, cut, and park

### Keep on the homepage

- Conversational surveys as a differentiated capture method
- Evidence / receipts
- Cross-source memory across calls, surveys, interviews, notes
- Story outputs: testimonials, impact reports, sponsor proof, internal alignment
- Decision support as a downstream outcome

### Cut from the homepage for now

- Personal task-organizer / "so many meetings and todos" framing
- Generic contact-memory assistant framing
- Sales playbook scoring / Gong-style QA framing
- "next best action" as the lead promise
- Anything that sounds like a broad CRM replacement

These can live on product pages or ICP pages later.

### Park as secondary or landing-page stories

- EOS / leadership-meeting prep
- founder-led seller revenue workflow
- donor / nonprofit-specific use cases
- event organizer wedge

They are valid, but they should route from the homepage, not define it.

## Homepage structure recommendation

### Section 1. Hero

Goal: make the category and outcome clear in under 10 seconds.

Suggested CTA pair:

- **See a real example**
- **Upload your first conversations**

Avoid generic "Learn more."

### Section 2. Why trust it

Show one concrete proof interaction:

- click a theme
- reveal the underlying quote, person, and source

If possible, show a survey answer, a call excerpt, and a synthesized theme in one frame.

### Section 3. How it works

Recommended three-step model:

1. **Capture** customer signal across calls, surveys, interviews, kiosk, and uploads
2. **Understand** themes, people, patterns, and missing signal
3. **Share and act** through story assets, aligned decisions, and follow-up

### Section 4. Why conversational surveys matter

This is one of the most important differentiated sections.

Recommended framing:

- generic surveys ask everyone the same questions
- UpSight starts with what you already know
- questions adapt to the person and the context
- respondents can answer in text, audio, or video
- you get richer evidence and more reusable story material

### Section 5. Outputs people actually want

Make the outputs tangible:

- themes your team can trust
- testimonial-ready quotes and clips
- impact-report evidence
- sponsor / donor proof
- clear next decisions for product, marketing, and programs

### Section 6. Audience routing

Route to pages for:

- Product / research teams
- Marketing / community teams
- Consultants / fractional leaders
- Events / program teams
- Revenue / EOS operators

This lets the homepage stay clear while acknowledging the broader product.

### Section 7. Operating cadence / EOS

Introduce only after the audience already understands the evidence layer.

Suggested framing:

**Once customer understanding is flowing, UpSight can help your team turn it into a weekly
operating rhythm.**

That is a much easier sell than leading with EOS in the hero.

## ICP recommendation

## The headline recommendation

**Do not let homepage positioning force you into a bad ICP.**

Homepage message and paid ICP do not have to be identical.

### Best-fit primary buying ICP for this homepage

The best commercial fit for this homepage story is:

**Lean B2B teams that already run customer conversations and need to turn them into aligned
decisions and reusable proof.**

In practice, that means:

- product / research / PMM teams at small and midsize B2B companies
- consultants and fractional leaders doing discovery and synthesis for clients
- founder-led teams sitting on existing calls and survey responses

What these groups have in common:

- they already have source material
- they feel the pain quickly
- they can see value from one good synthesis
- they care about both understanding and sharing

### Best-fit homepage proof audiences, but weaker primary ICPs

These belong on the page as examples, not as the main market:

- nonprofits needing better donor stories
- event organizers needing sponsor proof
- revenue teams wanting next-best-action

Why not lead with them:

- **nonprofits / donor teams** often have smaller budgets and slower cycles
- **events** can be seasonal and niche
- **sales workflow buyers** pull you back into CRM/Gong competition

### Specific recommendation

For the next homepage:

- **Lead promise:** customer intelligence
- **Primary commercial ICP:** lean B2B teams + consultants
- **Secondary proof stories:** nonprofit/donor, event/sponsor, revenue/EOS

## Unit-economics pressure test

## The key principle

If the homepage promise is **team understanding + story outputs**, this is **not** an
$29-per-seat-style product.

Why:

- value is shared across a workspace
- media capture and transcription are real variable costs
- the "story" outputs often imply richer analysis, exports, and collaboration
- the buyer is paying for aligned decisions and reusable proof, not personal productivity

### What looks dangerous

- Very low entry pricing with generous video/audio usage
- Unlimited seats plus unlimited analysis at a low flat price
- High-touch onboarding for tiny ACVs
- Leading with nonprofit/donor as the main market before cost discipline is proven

### What looks healthier

- workspace pricing, not strict seat pricing
- included usage tied to the expensive operations
- overages or capped metered usage for transcription / heavy analysis / media responses
- templates and self-serve onboarding, especially for survey -> synthesis -> share workflows

### Practical pricing recommendation

Based on the current story, I would pressure-test the existing low-end pricing assumptions.

**A $39 entry price may be too low** if it includes meaningful audio/video survey capture,
transcription, synthesis, and collaboration.

For this positioning, a healthier shape is likely:

- a free proof tier with tight limits
- a real paid workspace tier that feels like team software, not a consumer app
- metering on the genuinely expensive usage

Even if you keep a low sticker price for an entry tier, the included usage needs to be tight enough
that heavy story-collection customers are not structurally unprofitable.

### Who is most likely to work economically first

The strongest early unit-economics candidates are:

1. **Consultants / fractional operators**
   They have obvious ROI, fast evaluation cycles, and direct willingness to pay for synthesis and
   story outputs.
2. **Lean B2B product / PMM teams**
   Fewer accounts, but better expansion and collaboration value once adopted.
3. **Founder-led teams with existing call volume**
   Good fit if onboarding is simple and the first-value moment is fast.

The weakest early unit-economics candidate for a homepage-led motion is:

**nonprofits as a primary market**

Not because the need is fake. Because the budget tolerance and support burden can easily outrun the
ACV before the product is standardized.

## Critique of the current rough messaging notes

The strongest parts of the notes you provided are:

- **"know customers better first"** -> correct homepage priority
- **adaptive surveys based on prior interactions** -> genuinely differentiated
- **authentic media content for testimonials** -> commercially useful output, not fluff
- **on-site kiosk for fresh event feedback** -> differentiated capture story

The weakest parts are:

- too many different product promises in one pile
- several phrases drift into generic AI assistant territory
- some copy is feature-first instead of outcome-first
- donor / event / sales / task-management / personal CRM all appear at once

The homepage needs a single spine:

**Capture customer truth -> understand it -> share it -> act on it**

Everything else should either support that spine or move off the page.

## Recommended one-line positioning

If I had to pick one homepage one-liner right now:

**UpSight helps your team understand customers better and share their stories with confidence.**

More differentiated variant:

**UpSight turns customer conversations into evidence your team can trust and stories you can use.**

## V4 implementation note

The homepage now implements this strategy with a tighter conversion sequence. It uses
**customer intelligence for lean B2B teams** as the category-and-champion label, leads with
**Know your customers. Not just their feedback.**, and shows the multi-conversation proof in the
hero rather than treating it as a decorative lower-page video.

The page then contrasts five fragmented tools with one continuous customer-intelligence layer,
shows unify / understand / act, explains the persistent-memory mechanism, gives a three-step route
to first value, adds founder conviction, and repeats the concrete **Analyze 3 conversations** ask.
This preserves the customer-first homepage and keeps EOS as a later expansion story.
