### What this file is for

This is the research substrate for a GTM lane, not a grant-seeking guide. The **strategic order is
fixed and does not reverse**: *grant reporting is the wedge, early warning is the retention story.*
So the most valuable content here is not the money — it is the **recurring, funded, legally
mandated obligation to go collect data from businesses.** Where a program forces a grantee to
survey firms, document outcomes, or chase people for a number a year after they stopped serving
them, that is a product requirement, and it is written up in detail.

Where a program's reporting burden is financial — invoices, ledgers, certified payroll, books-and-
records reviews — it is noted briefly and marked as **not our wedge.** Being honest about that is
more useful than inflating the addressable surface.

# PART 1 — STATE PROGRAMS

## 1.1 California Competes Tax Credit (CCTC)

- **Agency:** Governor's Office of Business and Economic Development (GO-Biz)
- **Who applies:** **Businesses directly.** Any industry, size, or location. Cities and EDCs cannot apply on a business's behalf — an EDC's role here is referral and application support, which is a relationship play, not a reporting one.
- **Award size / funding:** "Over $180 million available in tax credits" annually *(sourced, GO-Biz page)*. Note: a separate LAO analysis and third-party coverage cite ~$308M and ~$120M figures for different fiscal years — **the annual pool moves and is set in the budget act. Verify the current year's number.**
- **Cycles / deadlines (FY 2026-27, sourced from the GO-Biz page 2026-09-23):**
  - July 20 – August 10, 2026 *(closed as of this writing)*
  - January 4 – January 25, 2027
  - March 1 – March 15, 2027

### Reporting and compliance obligations

- Agreements run **five years**, during which all employment and investment milestones must be achieved to claim the full credit, and those milestones must then be **maintained for three further years** — an eight-year tail.
- Each agreement carries **year-by-year milestones** for full-time employment, salary levels, and project investment, negotiated individually with GO-Biz. Milestones must be met **for each taxable year** to earn that year's allocation.
- **Annual notification:** where a taxpayer achieved the prior taxable year's milestones, it "shall notify GO-Biz on or before the first day of the fourth month after the close of each taxable year." For a calendar-year filer that is **April 1** *(derived)*.
- **Franchise Tax Board books-and-records review.** The GO-Biz page references an FTB Compliance Review Process document; FTB conducts books and records reviews of recipients.
- **Recapture:** every agreement contains a recapture provision. Failure to meet or maintain job-creation or investment milestones lets GO-Biz disallow the credits or require repayment of credits already claimed.

### Data-collection burden this creates

Headcount, FTE conversion, wage levels and capital expenditure, pulled annually from the company's own payroll and GL, defensible to an FTB examiner. **This is an accounting obligation, not a research one.**

- **Sources:** https://business.ca.gov/california-competes-tax-credit/ (accessed 2026-09-23); https://lao.ca.gov/Publications/Report/5162 (accessed 2026-09-23); https://www.ftb.ca.gov/about-ftb/data-reports-plans/california-competes-tax-credit-report-march-2026.pdf (accessed 2026-09-23)
- **Verdict: NOT our wedge.** Listed because EDCs are asked about it constantly and you need to speak to it credibly.

## 1.2 California Competes Grant (CCG / CCGP)

- **Agency:** GO-Biz
- **Who applies:** Businesses directly.
- **Eligibility thresholds:** commit to at least one of — create ≥500 new full-time California jobs; invest ≥$10 million in facility construction/renovation; or create the jobs / make the investment in an **area of high unemployment and/or high poverty** as defined in the California Competes regulations.
- **Award size:** third-party listings cite total funding of $120,000,000 with awards of $5M–$36M *(sourced to a grant-aggregator listing, not to GO-Biz directly — **verify with GO-Biz**)*.
- **Deadline:** **Not verified.** A "California Competes Grant Round 3" listing exists on the California Grants Portal; I could not confirm an open FY2026-27 round. Check https://www.grants.ca.gov/ and CalCompetes@gobiz.ca.gov / (916) 322-4051.

### Reporting and compliance obligations

Disbursement follows Exhibit A of the executed grant agreement once the California Competes Committee approves it. Milestone-and-recapture structure parallel to the tax credit. **Specific reporting terms live in the individual agreement and are not published** — I could not verify them.

- **Sources:** https://business.ca.gov/california-competes-tax-credit/ (accessed 2026-09-23); https://www.grants.ca.gov/grants/california-competes-grant-round-3/ (accessed 2026-09-23); https://business.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/CalCompetes-Grant-Solicitation-FY-2023-24.pdf (accessed 2026-09-23, **FY23-24 — superseded, structural reference only**)
- **Verdict: NOT our wedge**, same reasoning as 1.1.

## 1.3 CalOSBA Technical Assistance Program (TAP) — 2026-27 ⭐

**This is one of the three heaviest qualitative-reporting programs in the state, and its grantees are exactly the organizations in this GTM lane.**

- **Agency:** California Office of the Small Business Advocate (CalOSBA), within GO-Biz
- **Who applies:** **Nonprofits only** — "federally or privately funded small business technical assistance centers in California that provide free or low-cost one-on-one consulting and training to small businesses and entrepreneurs." Must be a federally designated TA center, a 501(c)(3), or a **501(c)(6)** *(the 501(c)(6) inclusion is what puts chambers of commerce in scope)*. Must be located in California and serve California small businesses. **Explicitly not a grant to small businesses.**
- **Funding:** $23,000,000 state General Fund for 2026-27.
- **Award size:** not fixed — "dependent on evaluation scores and budget reasonableness."
- **Match:** **100% match required** from an eligible federal or private funding source.
- **Cycle (sourced, grants.ca.gov, accessed 2026-09-23):**
  - Letter of Intent deadline: **July 31, 2026** *(closed)*
  - Full application deadline: **August 17, 2026, 4:59 PM** *(closed)*
  - Award announcement: **September 2026** — i.e. *right now*. **This is a live outreach trigger.**
  - Grant period: **October 1, 2026 – September 30, 2027**

### Reporting and compliance obligations — in detail

- **The TAP Data Model is mandatory for performance reporting.** Client demographics are defined in the TAP Performance Data Model. This is a prescribed schema, not a free-form narrative — grantees must produce exactly these fields.
- **Quarterly reporting and reimbursement**, both submitted through CalOSBA's online grantee portal. Payment is **cost-reimbursement**: CalOSBA pays within 45 calendar days of a complete, valid and undisputed invoice **with all required documentation and reporting**. *Late or incomplete performance data literally stops the money.* This is the single most important commercial fact about this program.
- **Unique-client counting rules:** metrics asking for "unique client" allow no duplication even where the client participated multiple times. *(This is a person-level identity-resolution problem, which is the shape of UpSight's core data model.)*
- **Training hours** counted as hours of delivery (a three-hour workshop = three training hours). Consulting is tracked by service category — business plans and strategy, capital readiness and fundraising, expansion and revenue growth strategies, financial management.
- **Monitoring:** applicants are subject to monitoring, **financial audits, and at least one site visit during the grant period.**

### The data-collection burden this creates

A TAP center must, **every quarter**, produce a deduplicated roster of every business it touched, with demographics of the owner, the service category, hours delivered, and the outcomes attributable to that help. Owner demographics are not in any accounting system — someone has to ask the business owner, get an answer, and keep it linked to that person across quarters and across grant years so the deduplication holds. Outcomes require going back to a business you may have advised eight months ago and asking what happened.

Practically: **an intake instrument at first contact, a follow-up instrument at some interval, and a persistent identity that survives between them.** That is the product.

- **Sources:** https://www.grants.ca.gov/grants/technical-assistance-program-tap-2026-2027/ (accessed 2026-09-23); https://calosba.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/TAP-2025-2026_Program-Announcement.pdf (accessed 2026-09-23, prior-year announcement — the Data Model and counting rules are quoted from here and **may have changed for 2026-27**); https://calosba.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/TAP2023_Frequently-Asked-Questions.pdf (accessed 2026-09-23, FY23 FAQ, structural only)
- **Verdict: PRIMARY wedge. Rank #2 overall (see §1).**

## 1.4 CalOSBA Capital Infusion Program (CIP) — 2026-27 ⭐

- **Agency:** CalOSBA / GO-Biz
- **Who applies:** **Only the five California SBDC lead center networks** — Northern CA, Central CA, Los Angeles, Orange County/Inland Empire, and **San Diego & Imperial**. One consolidated application per region. Explicitly not a grant for small businesses.
- **Funding:** $3,000,000 annually, ~5 awards (one per network). Purpose is to draw down federal SBA funds.
- **Match:** **100% — a 1:1 match of SBA funds**, evidenced by a "Certification of SBA Cash Match" form.
- **Cycle (sourced, grants.ca.gov, accessed 2026-09-23):**
  - Opens: **August 18, 2026** · Closes: **September 11, 2026, 4:59 PM** *(closed)*
  - Award announcement: September 2026 · Grant period: **October 1, 2026 – September 30, 2027**

### Reporting and compliance obligations

- **Performance metrics reported quarterly**, plus **mid-year and annual narrative reports**.
- Those narrative reports must **document advising activities and client outcomes, including the number and dollar amount of loans, equity investments, and other capital raised.**
- Quarterly cost-reimbursement, 45-day payment window, all reports and invoicing through the Grantee Portal.

### The data-collection burden this creates

"Dollar amount of capital raised" is information **only the business can give you, and only if it chooses to.** There is no ledger on the SBDC's side that contains it. Advisors chase founders by phone and email for months to close these numbers, and the narrative report then needs a story around them. This is the purest example in the whole document of *a funded compliance obligation that can only be satisfied by talking to people and recording what they said.*

Note the tight coupling: the San Diego & Imperial SBDC network is simultaneously a CIP grantee, an SBA cooperative-agreement holder (§2.3), and very likely a TAP grantee. **One organization, three overlapping reporting regimes, three different schemas, same underlying clients.** That is a wedge argument in one sentence.

- **Source:** https://www.grants.ca.gov/grants/capital-infusion-program-cip-2026-2027/ (accessed 2026-09-23)
- **Verdict: PRIMARY wedge.**

## 1.5 California Jobs First / Regional Investment Initiative (RII) — formerly CERF ⭐⭐

**The heaviest qualitative reporting burden I found anywhere in this research.**

- **Agencies:** GO-Biz, in partnership with EDD and the California Workforce Development Board, on behalf of the **Jobs First Council**. (Program history: launched 2021 as the Community Economic Resilience Fund / CERF under LWDA-EDD; renamed California Jobs First; the investment arm is now the Regional Investment Initiative.)
- **Who applies (Implementation Phase, sourced from the RFP):** nonprofits — 501(c)(3), (c)(4) **and (c)(6)** — and other community-based organizations; institutions of higher education or consortia; California Native American Tribes or consortia; local governments, special districts, MPOs. **Requires local partnerships and a regional coalition.** Only applications evidencing impact into **disinvested communities** are accepted.
- **Structure:** two-phase competition. Phase 1 selects regional coalitions and their "project clusters" (3–8 interconnected, implementation-ready projects under a single vision to grow one tradable sector from the State Economic Blueprint's "Accelerate" or "Bet" categories). Phase 2 evaluates and funds individual projects within the selected clusters, including a pitch interview.
- **Award size:** **minimum $500,000** per individual project; **no maximum**; **no matching funds requirement.** Administrative costs capped at **10%** of the total award.
- **Period of performance:** ~24 months; Round 2 anticipated start August 2026.
- **Funding and rounds (sourced):**
  - Up to $125M across two Implementation rounds.
  - **Round 1:** NOI 2025-03-26 · Pre-app 2025-04-23 · Full app 2025-05-28. **$80M awarded, announced 2025-08-29**, 11 projects, 18 counties, 7 regions, ~23,000 jobs claimed.
  - **Round 2:** NOI 2025-11-05 · Pre-app 2025-12-03 · **Full app 2026-01-16**. **$48.5M awarded, announced 2026-08-27**, 18 projects, 33 counties, ~20,000 jobs claimed.
  - Cumulative initiative investment stated as $434.5M since 2021.
  - Earlier phases: Planning (~$600M program envelope), and a **Catalyst phase** — $182M distributed by EDD across the 13 regional Jobs First organizations; individual Catalyst sub-awards of up to $400,000 with no minimum, open to CBOs, nonprofits, NGOs, labor organizations, academic institutions and local governments; Catalyst funds accessible through 2026-07-31 with the phase running to September 2026.
  - **A Round 3 is not announced.** *(inferred — not verified: given the cumulative total and the two-round $125M envelope, Implementation may be complete. Check labor.ca.gov and business.ca.gov before telling anyone a third round is coming.)*

### Reporting and compliance obligations — in detail

**Project reporting.** Awardees submit **quarterly reports assessing progress against the commitments in the grant agreement.** All Phase 2 full applications must include indicators and metrics that are "specific, measurable, and appropriate"; the final set is fixed in the grant agreement. **The applicant proposes its own metrics and is then held to them for two years** — which means a bad metric chosen at application time becomes a two-year data-collection liability.

**The sample metrics the RFP publishes are the tell.** Quoted from the Implementation RFP:

*Workforce Development*
- Total number of participants served (total, career services, job training)
- Total number and % of participants receiving training by Skill Type — Basic, Cross-functional, Knowledge-based
- % of total training participants who completed training
- Total number of participants trained **and placed**
- Number with a Credential Attainment; **% with a credential attainment within 1 year of program exit**
- % of graduates with **measurable skill gains**
- % of graduates **employed**
- **% of training graduates employed with health insurance benefits**
- **Median training graduates' quarterly earnings over time and compared to regional living wage targets (prior to training and after training)**

*Ecosystem Support*
- Anticipated new licensed technologies or patented innovation; new start-ups, spinouts, or exits
- **Number of small/midsize businesses in disinvested communities connected to universities, research centers or national labs**
- **Expected number of small/midsize businesses with access to funding or other assistance to overcome barriers to entry into new industries**
- Anticipated number of good-paying jobs

*Infrastructure*
- **Size and makeup of the population expected to benefit from the investment**
- Anticipated new businesses or expansion of businesses in disinvested communities
- Anticipated number of good-paying jobs

*Additional Enablers*
- **Number of network partners serving or representing members of disinvested communities**
- Number of new initiatives or partnerships contributing to social safety net programs
- Anticipated number of high-quality jobs

**Definitions that drive the data collection.** "Disinvested Communities" is defined broadly and, critically, **regionally** — the applicable regional Jobs First Strategy's own definition governs, which may include: census tracts flagged disadvantaged by CalEPA via **CalEnviroScreen 4.0** or by the federal **Climate and Economic Justice Screening Tool**; tracts at or below 80% of regional median household income or below HCD's state income limits; "high poverty area" and "high unemployment area" as designated under the **California Competes Tax Credit** program; California Native American Tribes per the NAHC Tribal Consultation Policy; and **individuals** who are people with disabilities, opportunity youth, first-generation students, or English language learners. "Good-paying jobs" is likewise defined by the applicable regional Jobs First Strategy — **it is not a statewide number.**

**Financial reporting.** Monthly financial reports and quarterly expenditure reports in GO-Biz-prescribed formats. Reimbursable grant — invoices plus supporting documentation for every expenditure. A **separate ledger per program** showing total funds available and monthly deductions; each entry needs subcontractor agreement number, invoice date, payment date, and a description. The Financial Report Template includes consultant names — **including affiliated organizations where the work is a partnership with a participating TAP center, chamber, accelerator, incubator, institution or government entity** — and descriptions of non-labor expenditures.

**Monitoring, procurement and records.**
- Monitored and/or audited by the state under State General Fund rules; awardees must give timely access to records, staff and subcontractors. GO-Biz and EDD **may require an audit by the state or a third party.**
- Procurement: **three competitive quotes** for purchases, kept on file against a monitoring visit; sole-source purchases require written justification.
- **Record keeping: project and fiscal records sufficient for state and local reviewers to evaluate the project's effectiveness and proper use of funds, with both original and summary (computer-generated) data sources. Retained five years from final payment** unless a longer period is stipulated. **The awardee — not GO-Biz, not any state agency — retains possession, custody and control of all records**, and must make them available for inspection and audit on request.
- Subrecipient risk assessment and ongoing monitoring proportional to assessed risk.
- Prevailing wage applies, with retention withheld pending compliance. Environmental compliance documentation (permits, surveys, reports) required.
- Intangible property: awardee takes title but must use it for the originally authorized purpose; the state reserves rights to **data produced under the award** and to authorize others to receive it.

### The data-collection burden this creates

This is the worst of it, and it is worth being concrete. To report "% of training graduates employed with health insurance benefits" and "median quarterly earnings prior to training and after training compared to regional living wage targets," a grantee must:

1. Capture a **baseline** earnings figure from each participant *before* training.
2. Track each participant through completion, credentialing and placement.
3. **Go find that person again — at exit, at one year after exit, and repeatedly across a 24-month performance period — and ask them whether they are employed, what they earn, and whether their job carries health insurance.**
4. Do the same for the business-side metrics: how many SMBs in disinvested communities got connected to a research institution, how many got access to funding. That is a survey of firms, not a query against a database.
5. Keep every one of those answers **linked to its source, for five years, in the grantee's own custody, ready for a state auditor** who will ask where a number came from.

There is **no state system of record for this.** CalJOBS covers WIOA participants, not RII project participants. Every awardee is building this from spreadsheets, an intake form, and whoever on staff is willing to make follow-up calls. **This is the wedge, stated in the funder's own words.**

- **Sources:** https://business.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Implementation-RFP.pdf (accessed 2026-09-23 — the metric lists, definitions, record-keeping and procurement text above are quoted from this document); https://www.labor.ca.gov/regional-investment-initiative/ (accessed 2026-09-23); https://business.ca.gov/california-awards-80-million-to-drive-economic-growth-and-spur-job-creation-across-the-state/ (accessed 2026-09-23); http://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/08/27/what-they-are-saying-leaders-from-across-the-state-celebrate-transformative-economic-investments-through-california-jobs-first/ (accessed 2026-09-23); https://www.labor.ca.gov/jobs-first-catalyst-phase-awardees/ (accessed 2026-09-23); https://edd.ca.gov/siteassets/files/unemployment/pdf/cerf-program-annual-report.pdf (accessed 2026-09-23)
- ⚠️ **The RFP linked above is the 2025 document governing Rounds 1 and 2.** Reporting terms are "finalized by an agreement between the awardee and GO-Biz," so actual obligations vary per award. Any future round will have its own RFP.
- **Verdict: PRIMARY wedge. Rank #1 overall.**

## 1.6 Employment Training Panel (ETP)

- **Agency:** Employment Training Panel (under the Labor & Workforce Development Agency)
- **Who applies:** Employers directly (Single Employer Contracts), **or Multiple Employer Contractors (MECs)** — and the MEC category explicitly includes **Chambers of Commerce, Trade Associations, Community Colleges, Professional/Trade Organizations, and Economic Development Corporations.** *An EDC or chamber can hold ETP money and run training on behalf of member firms — this makes ETP directly relevant to this lane, not just adjacent.*
- **Funding source:** the Employment Training Tax; **performance-based reimbursement** for the cost of training.
- **Cycle:** **Core "Contract Funds" are open on a rolling basis** via the Cal-E-Force portal as of 2026-09-23. Special solicitations (EV infrastructure, apprenticeship, social entrepreneurship, healthcare workforce) were all listed as **closed** on that date. A Paid Family Leave Small Business (PFL SB) solicitation for MECs was issued January 2026 — status not verified.
- **MEC economics (from the PFL SB solicitation):** MEC awardees may take a **maximum 13% administrative fee**, with an additional **12% in support costs**; all other funds must flow to eligible participating small businesses.

### Reporting and compliance obligations — in detail

- **Per-trainee ETP104 authorization form**, signed by the trainee, carrying their **Social Security Number**, giving ETP permission to access the trainee's confidential Unemployment Insurance information and employer-reported wage data from EDD. **The contractor is responsible for obtaining that signature.**
- **Trainee-level enrollment and training records** maintained by the contractor: who enrolled, what training, how many hours.
- **Records of trainee employment during training and during the retention period.**
- **Records of post-retention wage rates.**
- **Retention rule:** a business earns ETP funds only after a trainee completes all training **and is retained a minimum period (normally 90 calendar days)** at a required wage, in a job using the skills trained. Trainees must earn at least the ETP minimum wage after training plus retention. For **retrainees**, the post-retention wage must be **higher than the wage at the start of training**, except where a collective bargaining agreement precludes it.
- **Invoicing:** job closeout invoice once all trainees in a Job have completed training and been hired/retained for the specified period; **Final Fiscal Invoice within 30 days after the agreement end date.** Payment of the final invoice is contingent on submittal of applicable reports.
- **Audits:** ETP auditors obtain and review enrollment and training records, employment records during training and retention, and post-retention wage rates, verifying that trainees met retention and wage requirements as specified.

### The data-collection burden this creates

Heavy, but **structurally different from RII and TAP**: it is roster-and-attendance discipline plus employer wage confirmation, and the EDD UI wage match does much of the verification work. A MEC running training for 30 member firms must collect a signed, SSN-bearing form from every trainee, track attendance hours, and then confirm with each employer at day 90 that the person is still employed and at what wage.

- **Sources:** https://etp.ca.gov/fundingopportunities/ (accessed 2026-09-23); https://etp.ca.gov/planning-your-training/audit-process/ (accessed 2026-09-23); https://etp.ca.gov/program-overview/trainee-wages/ (accessed 2026-09-23); https://etp.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/70/2025/07/ETP_DetailedProgramOverview_July2025.pdf (accessed 2026-09-23); https://etp.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/70/2026/01/PFLSB4_SolicitationForProposals-SFP.pdf (accessed 2026-09-23)
- **Verdict: PARTIAL fit.** The employer-side follow-up ("is this person still here, at what wage, with what benefits") is conversational and does fit. The trainee compliance paperwork — ETP104s with SSNs — is regulated PII handling that UpSight should **not** touch.

## 1.7 IBank — Small Business Finance Center / Small Business Loan Guarantee Program (SBLGP)

- **Agency:** California Infrastructure and Economic Development Bank (IBank)
- **Who applies:** Businesses apply through participating lenders. The **grantee-equivalent reporting entities are the Financial Development Corporations (FDCs)** who administer the guarantees.

### Reporting and compliance obligations

FDCs are required **by statute (Gov. Code Art. 11 § 63089.97) and contractually** to report regularly to IBank on the status of enrolled guarantees:
- **Annual reports due to IBank by July 31**
- **Audited financial statements due by October 31** for the prior fiscal year
- **Quarterly "Default Recovery Report"** — or a nil report where there is no activity
- A **full staff roster**

### The data-collection burden this creates

Loan status, default and recovery, audited financials. **Jobs data is collected at loan enrollment**, but the recurring obligation is financial.

- **Sources:** https://ibank.ca.gov/small-business/loan-guarantees/ (accessed 2026-09-23); https://ibank.ca.gov/news-publications/reporting/ (accessed 2026-09-23); https://ibank.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/SBLGP-Policy-Manual-9.28.23.pdf (accessed 2026-09-23 — **2023 policy manual, may be superseded**); https://www.caloanmatch.org/ (accessed 2026-09-23)
- **Verdict: NOT our wedge.**

## 1.8 Caltrans Sustainable Transportation Planning Grants (STPG) — FY 2027-28 ⏰

**This is the nearest verified live deadline in this document.**

- **Agency:** Caltrans, Division of Transportation Planning
- **Who applies:** transit agencies, local governments, regional and metropolitan transportation planning organizations, and federally recognized Native American tribal governments. *EDCs and chambers participate as partners and as the people who run the business outreach, not usually as applicants.*
- **Funding (FY 2027-28):** ~**$34.9 million** total —
  - Sustainable Communities Grants: $30.4M ($17.9M competitive / $12.5M formula)
  - Strategic Partnerships Grants: $4.5M
- **Deadline: October 9, 2026, 5:00 PM** *(sourced — Caltrans news release 2026-018 and the STPG program page, both accessed 2026-09-23)*
- **Awards announced:** spring 2027.
- **Match:** a Local Match Calculator is published; the minimum percentage was not visible on the pages I read — **not verified, see the FY 2027-28 Application Guide.**

### Reporting and compliance obligations

- **Quarterly Progress Report** — Caltrans publishes a template for grantees.
- **Under-resourced community analysis is an application requirement with an evidentiary standard:** applicants must explain how the project affects under-resourced communities, **use the screening tools in Appendix A** to define the under-resourced community in the project area, **cite the data sources and tools used, and include a comparison to the statewide thresholds established in each tool.** "Under-resourced communities" are defined as the most vulnerable places facing disproportionate economic, environmental and health burdens.
- Charter cities are **not** exempt from the Annual Progress Report requirement.

### The data-collection burden this creates

Community and business engagement documentation. Corridor and main-street planning grants live or die on demonstrating that you actually talked to the merchants and residents on that corridor, and that you can show **who** you talked to and **what they said.** That is qualitative evidence with provenance — exactly the shape UpSight produces. The burden is front-loaded into the application and the quarterly narrative rather than being a multi-year chase.

## 1.11 EDD / WIOA and the local workforce boards

- **Agencies:** California Employment Development Department and the **California Workforce Development Board**, flowing WIOA Title I funds to **Local Workforce Development Boards** (in this region, the **San Diego Workforce Partnership**), who contract with subrecipient service providers.
- **Who applies:** service providers respond to local board solicitations. Boards themselves receive formula allocations.

### Reporting and compliance obligations — in detail

**The six WIOA primary indicators of performance (WIOA §116(b)(2)(A)) apply across core programs** to states *and to local areas*:
1. Employment (unsubsidized) in the **second quarter after exit** — for Title I Youth, education/training or unsubsidized employment
2. Employment in the **fourth quarter after exit**
3. **Median earnings** in the second quarter after exit
4. **Credential attainment**
5. **Measurable skill gains**
6. **Effectiveness in serving employers**

Employment includes unsubsidized employment, registered apprenticeship and military service. Measurement uses participant wage data tracked over time.

**Locally (San Diego Workforce Partnership):** the SDWP Operations Manual governs subrecipients, Career Center Operators, ETPL providers, and Customized Training / Contracted Education Training / On-the-Job Training providers. Subrecipients "must have the capability to report expenditures, participant data, and outcome data to the State in a manner that is timely, thorough, and accurate through **CalJOBS**, the state's required reporting system." Chapter 8 covers **oversight and monitoring**: review of all funded programs through monitoring visits and ongoing review to assess quality and performance.

### The data-collection burden this creates

Follow-up contact with participants **two and four quarters after they left the program** — literally calling people six months and a year later. Much of the employment and earnings verification is carried by UI wage record matching, which blunts the burden. But **"effectiveness in serving employers" is an employer-side measure** and there is no wage-record substitute for it — boards survey employers.

- **Sources:** https://www.dwd.wisconsin.gov/wioa/policy/11/11.5.htm (accessed 2026-09-23 — a state policy restatement of the federal indicators, used because it is a clean summary; **the authority is WIOA §116 and the federal joint regulations, not Wisconsin**); https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/eta/eta20250813 (accessed 2026-09-23); https://workforce.org/operations-manual/ (accessed 2026-09-23); https://workforce.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/SDWP-Operations-Manual-Chapter-8_Rev-January-2024.pdf (accessed 2026-09-23 — **revised January 2024, may be superseded**)
- **Verdict: PARTIAL fit.** CalJOBS is the mandated system of record — UpSight cannot replace it and should not try. The openings are employer-side satisfaction and effectiveness, and the qualitative narrative that CalJOBS does not hold.

## 2.2 CDBG — Community Development Block Grant (HUD), and its business-assistance uses ⭐

Two delivery channels matter here, and they have different applicants:

**(a) Entitlement CDBG** — large cities and urban counties receive funds directly from HUD (the **City of San Diego** is an entitlement grantee, see §3.1).
**(b) State CDBG** — **California HCD** administers funds for **non-entitlement jurisdictions**: cities under 50,000 population, counties with under 200,000 in unincorporated areas, and entities not in the HUD entitlement program. Non-federally-recognized Native American communities and Colonias may apply as partners with an eligible jurisdiction.

- **HCD cycle (sourced, hcd.ca.gov, accessed 2026-09-23):** the **2025 NOFA** portal opened **February 2, 2026, 9:00 AM PST**; applications closed **April 3, 2026, 3:00 PM PST**; award letters signed **September 2026**. **NOFA Amendment #1 was issued 2025-12-15** — a live example of mid-cycle change. **The next NOFA's timing is not verified.**
- **Economic development activity categories:** **Business Assistance (BA)** and **Microenterprise Assistance (ME)**.

### Reporting and compliance obligations — in detail

**National objective.** Every CDBG activity assisting a for-profit business for economic development / job creation or retention must meet one of three national objectives: benefit low- and moderate-income (LMI) persons; prevent or eliminate slums or blight; or meet an urgent community need. Overall, **reports must show that at least 70% of all CDBG expenditures benefit LMI persons.**

**Job creation — the documentation standard.** The grantee **and the assisted business** must document that permanent jobs have been created, and that **at least 51% of the jobs, computed on a full-time equivalent basis, involve the employment of LMI persons.** Documentation for each assisted business must include **income verification.** Family Income forms are required, documenting household income against current HUD income limits.

**Job retention.** To count retained jobs there must be "clear and objective evidence that permanent jobs will be lost without CDBG assistance" — for example a notice to employees or a public announcement by the business.

**Public benefit standards (24 CFR 570.209).** Activities must, **in the aggregate**, create or retain **at least one full-time-equivalent permanent job per $35,000 of CDBG funds used**, or alternatively provide goods or services to an area such that there is **at least one LMI person residing in the service area per $350 of CDBG funds used.** For entitlement cities and urban counties the aggregate standard applies to all applicable activities for which CDBG funds are first obligated within each program year.

**Microenterprise (ME) — the sharpest obligation.** A microenterprise is a business with **five or fewer employees including the owner(s).**
- **Income self-certification for the owner must be completed BEFORE the beneficiary receives any microenterprise technical assistance or support services.** Not after. Not at reporting time.
- Acceptable documentation for a microenterprise owner's income includes **Form 1040 tax forms and business tax filings showing the business's income.**
- Any microenterprise receiving **direct financial assistance** must be income-qualified under the full Part 5 process **and** must have **third-party documentation of having five or fewer employees (including owners) prior to receiving any financing.**
- Direct-benefit activities documented under LMI — including economic development loans and microenterprise assistance — require **full income determination and documentation**, not sampling.

**Reporting and retention.**
- Beneficiary accomplishments are reported in **IDIS**, HUD's Integrated Disbursement and Information System, and roll up annually into the jurisdiction's **CAPER**.
- **Records retained at least five years after the State closes out that year's funding award with HUD** (state CDBG).
- Ongoing compliance monitoring of subrecipients; application certifications and statement of assurances; governing-body resolution.

### The data-collection burden this creates

This is the most **invasive** collection in the document. To run a CDBG microenterprise program a city or its nonprofit subrecipient must ask a business owner for their **household income and tax returns before giving them an hour of free advice** — and then, if jobs are the national objective, collect **income verification from every person that business hires.** The refusal rate is the operational problem, and the awkwardness of the ask is why programs under-report.

A qualification: **most of this is eligibility documentation at intake, not longitudinal outcome research.** It is forms and tax returns, and there are established (if hated) workflows for it. What UpSight would add is the layer *around* it — the follow-up on what actually happened to the business, the narrative for the CAPER, and the evidence that survives a monitoring visit.

- **Sources:** https://www.hud.gov/hud-partners/community-cdbg · https://www.hcd.ca.gov/funding/cdbg · https://www.hcd.ca.gov/sites/default/files/docs/grants-and-funding/cdbg/Appendix-D-Economic-Development-Manual.pdf · https://www.hcd.ca.gov/sites/default/files/docs/grants-and-funding/CDBG/c-6-economic-development-guidance.pdf · https://www.hcd.ca.gov/sites/default/files/docs/grants-and-funding/CDBG/D-3-Microenterprise-Income-Certification-508.pdf · https://www.hcd.ca.gov/grants-funding/already-have-funding/docs/meeting_low_mod_income_requrements_for_cdbg_activities.pdf · https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/subtitle-B/chapter-V/subchapter-C/part-570/subpart-C/section-570.209 · https://www.hudexchange.info/programs/cdbg-cv/cdbg-cv-toolkit/documentation/income/ (all accessed 2026-09-23)

## 2.3 SBA — Small Business Development Centers (SBDC) ⭐

- **Agency:** U.S. Small Business Administration, Office of Small Business Development Centers
- **Who holds the award:** university and nonprofit **lead centers** under a negotiated **Cooperative Agreement** with SBA; governed by **13 CFR Part 130**. Regionally, the **San Diego & Imperial SBDC Network**. State match flows through CalOSBA's CIP (§1.4).

### Reporting and compliance obligations — in detail

- **SBA Form 641 — Counseling Information / Client Intake Form.** A **signed** client intake form completed **in each instance of assistance**, feeding SBA's management information database. Collects client and business characteristics.
- **SBA Form 888 — Management Training Form.** Collects and reports information on classroom-style training.
- Together, 641 and 888 "are used to collect **counseling, training and economic impact information** from SBA Resource Partners and contractors that deliver business technical assistance," to understand the outputs and outcomes realized.
- **System of record: Nexus CRM has replaced EDMIS** as the system of record for Resource Partner client activity and training data. Partners can upload 641 and 888 data from third-party systems or enter it directly. *(This matters: SBA explicitly contemplates third-party systems feeding it — an integration surface, not a walled garden.)*
- **Annual goal numbers are negotiated with the local district director** during proposal submission and, once approved by OSBDC, are **incorporated into the Notice of Award** — meaning the center is contractually bound to hit numbers it has to collect from clients.
- **Economic impact measures** (capital infusion, jobs, sales change) are collected. An annual client impact survey is part of the SBDC performance regime; **the current instrument and contractor are NOT verified** — ask a lead center.
- SBA issues an **SBDC Cooperative Agreement Monitoring Statement**; centers are also subject to the programmatic reviews in 13 CFR 130.

### The data-collection burden this creates

Every counseling session produces a signed form. Then, separately and later, someone has to go back to the client and ask: **did you get the loan, how much, did your sales change, did you hire.** Clients who got what they needed have no incentive to answer. Advisors are measured on numbers they cannot obtain without a relationship and a follow-up conversation.

## 2.5 Single Audit — the threshold that changes the conversation

Not a program, but the compliance fact that governs how seriously any of these organizations take their records.

- **2 CFR § 200.501:** an entity that expends **$1,000,000 or more in federal awards during its fiscal year** must obtain a **single audit** (or, in limited single-program situations, a program-specific audit).
- **The threshold was raised from $750,000 to $1,000,000**, effective **for fiscal years beginning on or after October 1, 2024.**
- ⚠️ **Awards issued before October 1, 2024 may remain subject to the old $750,000 threshold** — an organization with multi-year grants straddling that date can still be pulled into a Single Audit below $1M. *(This dual position was flagged in professional commentary; I have not verified it against OMB text — confirm with the organization's auditor.)*
- Related 2024 changes: **de minimis indirect cost rate raised from 10% to 15%** of modified total direct costs; **Type A program threshold to $1,000,000** for entities with ≤$34M total federal awards expended; **subaward MTDC exclusion from $25,000 to $50,000**; **equipment capitalization floor from $5,000 to $10,000.**
- ⚠️ **An OMB Uniform Guidance overhaul is reported to be in motion for 2026.** I did not read it. **Verify before citing any of the above.**
- **Submission deadline** is set by 2 CFR 200.512 — **not verified here.**
- **Sources:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/2/200.501 · https://support.fac.gov/hc/en-us/articles/18792372809101-Is-my-organization-required-to-conduct-a-Single-Audit · https://www.cbiz.com/insights/article/2024-uniform-guidance-changes-requirements-for-single-audits · https://www.peasebell.com/insights/omb-2026-uniform-guidance-overhaul/ (all accessed 2026-09-23)

# ANALYSIS

## 1. The reporting-burden ranking — the product opportunity, ranked

Ranked by **how painful the recurring collection of data *from businesses and people* is** — not by dollars, and not by total compliance weight. Financial reporting is deliberately discounted.

### #1 — California Jobs First / RII Implementation Phase

**Why it is worst:** the metrics the state publishes as examples are not measurable from any administrative system. "% of training graduates employed with health insurance benefits." "Median training graduates' quarterly earnings over time and compared to regional living wage targets, prior to training and after training." "Number of small/midsize businesses in disinvested communities connected to universities, research centers or national labs."

Each of those requires a **baseline capture, a longitudinal follow-up, and a person you have to find again.** Reported **quarterly, across a 24-month performance period**, against metrics **the awardee itself proposed in its application and is now locked to.** Records must be retained **five years from final payment, in the awardee's own custody**, with original *and* summary data sources, available to a state auditor on request. And there is **no state system of record** — CalJOBS does not cover these participants. Every one of the 29 funded projects across Rounds 1 and 2 is solving this in a spreadsheet right now.

**The specific pain to name in a sales conversation:** *"Your grant agreement says you'll report median quarterly earnings before and after training, compared to a regional living wage. Who is calling those people a year later, and where does their answer live?"*

### #2 — CalOSBA TAP (with CIP alongside it)

**Why:** the **TAP Data Model is mandatory and prescriptive**, requires **client demographics** that only the business owner can supply, enforces **unique-client deduplication across the whole grant year**, and is filed **quarterly on cost-reimbursement — meaning incomplete data stops the payment.** Add **at least one site visit and financial audit** per grant period. CIP piles on **mid-year and annual narrative reports documenting client outcomes including the number and dollar amount of loans, equity investments and other capital raised** — a figure that exists nowhere except in the founder's head until someone asks.

**The specific pain:** the deduplicated-unique-client requirement is an identity-resolution problem across quarters and channels. That is UpSight's core data model, not a feature.

**Commercial note:** $23M of TAP money went out in awards announced **September 2026**, with grant periods starting **October 1, 2026**. Those grantees are setting up their reporting *this month*.

### #3 — EDA ED-917 / ED-918 (and ED-915)

**Why:** the **five-year annual reporting tail** on outcomes attributable to a grant whose work finished years ago. ED-915 stretches to **3, 6 and 9 years** and asks about jobs at *subsequent employers who located or expanded in the project area as a result of your project* — attribution about firms you never served. EDA collects it through **Qualtrics**, which is EDA conceding it is a survey problem. Meanwhile EDDs must produce an **annually updated CEDS and an Annual Performance Report subject to public review and comment.**

**The specific pain:** institutional memory loss. By year four the staff have turned over and the engagement records are in a departed employee's inbox. *"What do you do when EDA asks you in 2029 about a program you ran in 2025?"*

### #4 — CDBG economic development and microenterprise assistance

**Why:** the most **invasive** ask in the document — **household income and tax returns from a business owner before you may give them assistance**, plus income verification for every person hired to prove 51% LMI on an FTE basis, against a public benefit standard of **one FTE job per $35,000** or **one LMI person served per $350**. Five-year retention. IDIS and CAPER reporting. Monitoring visits.

**Why it ranks below the top three:** most of it is **eligibility documentation captured at intake** — structured forms and tax returns with established (if hated) workflows — rather than longitudinal qualitative follow-up. The collectible-by-UpSight part is what happened *after*, and the narrative that has to survive a monitoring visit.

### #5 — Main Street / downtown district reinvestment statistics

**Why it is here despite tiny budgets:** "net new businesses," "net new jobs," "private reinvestment dollars" and "volunteer hours" for a commercial district, **annually, by hand, from every merchant on the street.** No system produces these. One or two staff walk the district or email 120 businesses. The pain-to-budget ratio is extreme in both directions: **maximum manual pain, minimum money.** Treat as design-partner and reference territory, not ACV.

### #6 — WIOA / local workforce boards

**Why it ranks here despite being genuinely onerous:** the **2nd- and 4th-quarter-after-exit** follow-up is real human chasing, but **UI wage-record matching carries most of the employment and earnings verification**, and **CalJOBS is a mandated system of record** that UpSight cannot and should not displace. The genuine opening is the sixth indicator — **"effectiveness in serving employers"** — which has no wage-record substitute and is surveyed.

### #7 — ETP

**Why lower:** heavy but **structured** — trainee rosters, attendance hours, signed ETP104s, 90-day retention and post-retention wage confirmation. The **EDD UI wage match does the verification**, and the ETP104 with its SSN is regulated PII that UpSight should stay away from. The fit is the employer-side confirmation conversation, not the compliance file.

### #8 — Caltrans STPG and placemaking grants

**Why lower:** genuinely qualitative — community and business engagement documentation, under-resourced-community justification with cited tools and statewide threshold comparisons — but **episodic** (per application, plus quarterly narratives) rather than a recurring multi-year obligation.

### Not ranked — CalCompetes (tax credit and grant), IBank SBLGP, Section 108 financial underwriting

**Payroll, GL, audited financials, default recovery, certified payroll.** Accounting obligations. **Not our wedge.** Say so plainly; credibility comes from the disqualification.

## 2. The common denominator — the spec for a product

Strip the program names away and nearly every entry above demands the same underlying record. **This is the data model.**

### A. Business identity and firmographics
Legal and DBA name · address and **census tract** (everything geographic resolves to a tract) · NAICS or sector · year founded · **FTE employee count** (FTE conversion is required by CDBG and implied everywhere) · revenue band · ownership structure · ≤5 employees flag (CDBG microenterprise)

### B. Owner and participant demographics
Race/ethnicity · gender · veteran status · disability status · **household income relative to HUD/HCD limits (LMI)** · preferred language · first-generation / opportunity-youth / English-language-learner status (RII's disinvested-community definition reaches individuals, not just tracts)

### C. Geography and equity flags — always tract-anchored
**CalEnviroScreen 4.0** · federal **Climate and Economic Justice Screening Tool** · HUD **LMI** area · **high-poverty / high-unemployment area** as defined under the California Competes regulations · tribal lands (NAHC) · Promise Zone / Opportunity Zone · ≤80% of regional median household income · the **region's own** Jobs First definition of "disinvested"

*Note the trap: **"disadvantaged" is not one definition.** Six programs above use six overlapping screens, and RII explicitly defers to the regional collaborative's local definition. A product that stores a tract and can evaluate it against multiple screens is worth more than one that stores a boolean.*

### D. Jobs — created, retained, and quality
Jobs created · jobs retained (**with evidence they would otherwise be lost**) · **FTE conversion** · full-time vs part-time · **wage level** · **health insurance / benefits** (RII asks explicitly) · **LMI status of each person hired** (CDBG's 51% test) · demographics of hires · **jobs at subsequent employers attributable to the project** (EDA ED-915) · comparison to a **regional living wage** (RII) or **ETP minimum wage** (ETP)

### E. Earnings, over time
Baseline earnings before intervention · **median quarterly earnings after** · earnings at 2 and 4 quarters after exit (WIOA) · **post-retention wage higher than pre-training wage** (ETP retrainees) · comparison to a regional threshold

### F. Capital and investment
Loans received — **count and dollar amount** · equity investments · grants · other capital raised · source · **private investment leveraged** · public reinvestment dollars (Main Street)

### G. Business outcomes
Started / expanded / relocated / closed · **survival** · revenue or sales change · contracts won · new markets entered · buildings rehabilitated · connections made (to universities, labs, capital, customers)

### H. Service dosage and attribution
Hours of one-on-one consulting · training hours delivered · **unique vs. repeat client, deduplicated across the reporting year** · service category · date of each engagement · **which grant funded it** (one client, many funding sources, different reporting rules)

### I. Narrative and verbatim
**What changed, and why the business owner says it changed.** Every program above has a narrative section, an impact story, or a "demonstrated results" criterion. This is the field nobody has a system for — it lives in advisors' memories and inbox threads.

### J. Provenance — the part that makes the rest defensible
Who said it · when · through what channel (call, survey, interview, site visit, email) · **consent and authorization on file** · **retained five years** (RII: from final payment; CDBG: after HUD closeout; **in the grantee's own custody**) · **available for inspection and audit on request** · original *and* summary data sources both preserved (RII's explicit wording)

> **The one-sentence spec:** *a persistent, person- and business-level profile carrying demographics,
> geography, jobs, wages, capital and outcomes, updated through repeated contact over five or more
> years, where every field traces back to a dated, attributed, consented statement by a named person.*

That is a description of UpSight with the compliance vocabulary swapped in.

## 3. Where UpSight plausibly fits — and where it does not

### Where it fits, strongest first

**1. The five-year persistent profile.** EDA's ED-917/918 five-year tail, ED-915's 3/6/9-year intervals, RII's 24-month performance period plus five-year retention, TAP's cross-quarter unique-client deduplication. **Every one of these is the same problem: the same business shows up years apart and must be recognised as the same business, with everything previously learned still attached.** Person-level profiles that persist are not a feature here — they are the requirement.

**2. Verbatim-linked evidence as audit defensibility.** RII requires **original and summary data sources**, five-year retention **in the awardee's custody**, and records **sufficient for reviewers to evaluate the project's effectiveness** — available on request. CDBG requires documentation per assisted business. TAP includes a site visit and a financial audit. When a monitor asks *"where did this number come from,"* the answer "here is the recorded conversation, the date, the person, and their exact words" is qualitatively better than a spreadsheet cell. **This is the single most defensible claim UpSight can make in this market**, and it is the claim no general-purpose CRM or grant-management tool makes.

**3. Unifying calls, surveys, interviews and notes.** Grantees collect through all four channels today — an intake form, an advisor's notes, a follow-up phone call, an annual survey — and **none of it reconciles.** That is precisely the fragmentation UpSight was built to close. It is also why the narrative sections of these reports are so bad: the story lives in one system and the numbers in another.

**4. The narrative and impact sections.** Every program has one. Nobody has a system for it. This is where UpSight's synthesis is straightforwardly better than the status quo, which is a director writing from memory at 11pm the night before a deadline.

**5. Equity and disadvantaged-community evidence.** Programs increasingly want **benefit to disinvested communities demonstrated, not asserted**, against multiple shifting definitions. Evidence from named people in named tracts, retrievable and quotable, is what that demand actually requires.

**6. Employer-side and business-side survey instruments.** WIOA's "effectiveness in serving employers," Main Street's annual merchant canvass, CIP's capital-raised chase, SBDC's client impact follow-up. All are surveys of businesses that somebody is running badly in a Google Form today.

### Where it does not fit — say this out loud

**Financial and accounting reporting is not our wedge, and pretending otherwise will lose deals.** Explicitly out of scope:
- Invoices, cost-reimbursement packages, RII's Financial Report Template, separate ledgers per program, monthly financial and quarterly expenditure reports
- Single Audit, audited financial statements, IBank FDC financial reporting
- Procurement documentation — three competitive quotes, sole-source justifications
- Davis-Bacon and prevailing wage, certified payroll
- FTB books-and-records reviews, CalCompetes milestone accounting
- Environmental review, historic preservation clearance

**UpSight is not the system of record where the funder mandates one.** **CalJOBS** (WIOA), **Nexus CRM** (SBA Resource Partners), **IDIS** (CDBG/HUD), **Cal-E-Force** (ETP), the **CalOSBA grantee portal**, EDA's **Qualtrics** collection. These are not competitors to displace — they are **destinations to feed.** SBA explicitly supports Resource Partners uploading 641/888 data from third-party systems, which is the integration story handed to us. **Position UpSight as the collection and evidence layer upstream of the mandated system, never as a replacement for it.** Any pitch that sounds like "replace CalJOBS" ends the meeting.

**UpSight should not handle the regulated compliance paperwork.** ETP104 forms carrying Social Security Numbers. CDBG household income verification with 1040s. That is PII with its own handling regime and its own vendors. **Adjacent to our data, and deliberately not ours.** Refusing that scope is a trust signal, not a gap.

**The honest risk to hold in view.** Several of these organizations already own a CRM — Salesforce Nonprofit Cloud, Neoserra (widely used in the SBDC world), Outcome Tracker, Apricot. The question they will ask is *"how is this different from the CRM we already hate?"* The answer has to be the evidence layer — verbatim, provenance, five-year persistence, cross-channel — and not "a better CRM." **Validate this objection in discovery before building anything.**
